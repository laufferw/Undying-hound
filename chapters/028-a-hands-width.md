# **Chapter 28: A Hand's Width**

Romeo did not look up at the grating. He did not let his ears move, or his tail, which had already moved once tonight without asking and was now on notice. A wolf the size of a hay cart had just told him, in a voice like snow sliding off a roof, that the man folded in the drain beside him was a liar, and the correct thing to do with that information was nothing at all. Not yet.

Brow lifted his head away from the bars. The cold went with him. Somewhere above, straw rustled as a very large animal lay down again and pretended to have been asleep for hours.

Ah-Shu had not noticed. He was wiping his face with the back of his wrist, the way men do when they are ashamed of having needed to.

*Lying about the door,* Romeo thought. *Which door?*

There had been a lot of doors lately.

---

He did what he had been doing for three thousand years when a man said one thing and smelled of another. He went back to the beginning and listened again, with his nose.

It was not difficult to make Ah-Shu tell the story twice. Romeo simply did not leave. He lay down in the silt, as close to the bare hand as a dog could lie without touching it, put his chin on his paws, and looked up with his head very slightly tilted. It is the oldest question a dog can ask, and it means *and then?*

"You want to hear it again," the man said. He gave a small, wet laugh. "Of course you do. Fine. It knocked. It knocked like my mother."

Fear, steady, a day old. True.

"It called me what she called me."

Grief, raw and fresh, because he could say it now: *Ah-Shu.* True.

"And I didn't open."

There.

It was not much. A lie told by a frightened man to a dog in a drain is not a grand thing, and it has no patience in it and no pleasure. It was small and sour and close to the skin, the way a child smells when he swears he didn't touch the honey jar with honey on his chin. Shame. And under the shame, faint as the last warmth in a bowl of ashes, something Romeo knew very well by now and would have given a great deal never to smell again.

Silk. Lamp smoke. Silver. The hole.

It was on the man's right hand. Not in the creases, with the frayed name-edges of wolf and fox and stoat. On the back of it, across the knuckles, in the shape of a grip. As though something had once taken that hand in its own and held it, gently, the way a mother holds a small boy's hand to walk him home across a field.

Romeo's hackles rose all along the red saddle of his back before he could stop them.

"What?" said Ah-Shu. He had felt the change in the dog the way soldiers feel weather. "What is it? What did I—"

And his right hand moved.

He did not move it. Romeo saw that clearly, and smelled it more clearly still: no intent went with it, none of the small sharp scent a body makes when it decides to reach for something. The hand simply came off the floor of the drain, palm down, fingers curling, and reached for the back of Romeo's neck the way you reach for a collar.

From above.

Romeo flinched so hard that his whole long body went sideways into the drain wall, and his back said something so loud and so final that for a moment there was nothing in the world but white. When it came back, he was flat on his belly a body's length away with his lip lifted off every one of his teeth, and he was growling. Not a puppy's growl. The other kind. The kind that had once made a tiger reconsider its afternoon.

Ah-Shu had his right wrist clamped in his left hand. He was staring at it. The fingers were still curling, slowly, the way a plant turns toward a window.

"I didn't," he whispered. "I didn't, I didn't, that wasn't—"

*No,* thought Romeo, through the white ringing in his spine. *That one wasn't you.*

He let the growl run down. He did not let his lip down. He looked at the man, and waited, and the man understood that he was being waited for.

---

"I opened it," Ah-Shu said.

The shame went out of his smell all at once, like a held breath let go. What was left was only fear, and grief, and something tired and sore that might have been relief.

"A crack. A hand's width. Not to let her in. I knew it wasn't her. My mother's been dead twenty-two years, I carried her to the hill myself. I knew." He pressed his right hand flat against his knee and held it there with his left. "But it was snowing, and she said she was cold. She said, *Shu, it's so cold out here, give me your hand at least.* And it was her voice. You don't— I don't know if you've ever heard someone again, after. You don't know what you'll do."

Romeo had heard someone again. Last night, under a floor, saying *Sit*.

"So I put my hand out through the crack." He swallowed. "And she held it. It was cold. It didn't feel like anything, it felt like holding the wind, but it was the way she held it. And she said—" His voice failed. He tried again. "She said, *There's my good boy. Will you bring me the little dog, Shu? Up the passage, on the dark night. And I'll give you back what's yours.*"

In the far stall the fox turned over in her straw and whimpered once in her sleep.

"And I said yes." Ah-Shu shut his eyes. "I didn't decide to. It was out of my mouth before I knew it was in it. Then I shut the door and sat against it till morning, and in the morning I went to say my name, and it wasn't there." He opened his eyes and looked at the dog in the dark. "I came to tell you about the hat because it's true, and because I wanted it to be true that I came here for you and not for her. That's all. I don't know any more which one it is. I don't know what my hand will do on the night."

Above them, very softly, Brow's voice came again, and only to Romeo.

*Now he is not lying.*

---

It is a strange thing to be very old in a very small body. You have far more thoughts than room to keep them in.

Romeo lay in the silt and had all of them at once.

He thought: *The stranger does not need to come to the den again. It has sent a man to fetch me.*

He thought: *It takes a name a little at a time, and a promise all at once, and it is cheaper to borrow a soldier's hand than a dead man's walk.*

He thought, and did not like thinking it: *The collector wears grey gloves. Always.*

And under all of those, the oldest thought, the one with Shen's voice in it: *Don't look for what's there. Look for the pot.* The pot, in that story, had been under the plum tree with a crow on it, in the one place nobody looked because everybody was watching the crow.

If the stranger expected the little dog to be led up the dry passage on the dark night, then on the dark night the stranger would be in the passage. Waiting at the top, by its room full of borrowed coats. Not in the den. Not under Ling Mei's board. Perhaps not even at the stone.

That was not a trap. Or rather it was, but a trap is only a trap if you don't know where it is. If you do, it is a map.

He dragged himself back to the silt where the old character for *millet* still showed in the faint light from the grating, and his back filed a formal complaint, and he overruled it. Beside *millet* he scratched one more character. It was only three strokes. In the old script it had meant *yes*, and also, depending on how you read it, *I have heard you*.

Ah-Shu traced it, frowning, his left hand still holding his right. His hand knew the shape, the way it had known *millet*.

"Yes," he said slowly. "You'll still come? Knowing?"

Romeo looked at him. Then, deliberately, he set one forepaw on the back of the man's right hand, flat, pinning it to his knee. It was cold under his pad, colder than skin should be, with that faint wrong smell across the knuckles. He held it there for the space of three breaths, to say *I know what this is*. Then he took his paw away.

"Knowing," said Ah-Shu. He let out a long breath. "All right. All right. Then I'll keep my hand in my belt until the dark night, and if it does anything I'll break my own fingers."

Romeo thought that was excessive, and also a very soldier's thing to say, and also not the worst plan anyone had made that week.

*Small dog,* said Brow from above, mind to mind, in the tone of a great wolf observing a puppy approach a beehive. *You are going to do something stupid.*

*I am going to consult a turtle first,* said Romeo, with dignity. *That is the difference between stupid and old.*

The wolf said nothing to that. But the straw above the grating rustled in a way that, in a smaller and less dignified animal, Romeo might have called a snort.

---

The way back was the same forty paces of tunnel and length of ditch, and it took him three times as long. He went on his belly and stopped often, and twice he lay in the trench with his chin in the snow and simply breathed, the slow nose-breathing Shen had taught him in a field where a viper might be sleeping, until the white went out of his spine.

*Yes,* he told the panel. *Thank you. I had noticed.*

The panel, being the System, did not say *you're welcome*. But it went away a little faster than usual, which he chose to take as tact.

When he slid down into the round chamber the trough was no longer frosted, and the den smelled of nothing worse than earth and stoat and himself. He did not trust it. Before he lay down he went to the mouth of the seam tunnel, the stranger's road, and with his nose and his front paws he pushed loose silt and pebbles into the first arm's length of it. Not enough to block it; nothing he could dig in his condition would stop something that drained away like water from a footprint. Enough that anything coming down it would have to move earth. Enough that he would hear.

Then he lay down across the tunnel mouth with his chin on his paws, the way a dachshund lies at a sett.

"You're back," said the floor.

*I said I would be.*

"You said a lot of things." A pause, and the creak of a girl who had been sitting on a board for two nights shifting her weight. "Was it a trap?"

*Yes,* said Romeo. *A useful one. I'll tell you in the morning.*

"Tell me now."

*Ling Mei.* He made his inner voice as gentle as it went, which was not very; it had been made for other things. *Nothing is coming through your floor tonight. It has sent someone else to fetch me, and it is waiting for him to do it, and he isn't going to do it tonight. I am lying across its road. I have very long ears. Go to sleep.*

Silence. Then: "On the board?"

*On the board, if you like. On the platform, if you'd rather. I'll hear either.*

She did not answer. But after a while he heard a blanket being pulled close, and a body folding down sideways onto the floor, right on the third floorboard, and a breath going long and slow and then catching on a small snore.

Romeo lay in the dark with his ears spread out on either side of his head like two dropped gloves and listened to her sleep, and listened to the seam tunnel not move, and drew earth qi up his spine one vertebra at a time. Somewhere toward the end of the night he must have slept too, because his paws were twitching when he woke, and he could not remember what he had been chasing.

---

It was nearly noon on the tenth day before anyone knocked, and it was only a fist on the hut door, in no pattern at all. Romeo was on his feet with his lip up before he had decided anything.

"Ling Mei?" A thin, anxious voice. Boiled turnip, ink, well rope. The junior steward. "Ling Mei, are you— you're asleep. You're asleep on the *floor*. Get up. Get up, please."

The board creaked as she sat up, groggy and furious. "I drew twenty-four buckets. You wrote it in the book. You said 'Well.'"

"I know. It's not buckets." The steward's voice dropped, as if the lane might hear. "It's Elder Fang. He's sent for you. Now, this afternoon, to the Beast Hall."

Below the floor, Romeo went very still.

"Me?" said Ling Mei. "Why would Elder Fang want—"

"Not just you," said the junior steward miserably. "He said to bring the sausage."
