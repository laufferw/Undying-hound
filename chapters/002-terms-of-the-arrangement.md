# **Chapter 2: Terms of the Arrangement**

Ling Mei's hut was the last one on the last row of the outer disciples' quarter, where the path gave up being a path and became a ditch. It had one room, one window with a paper screen that was more hole than paper, and a roof that let the rain in at three places. She had set out a bowl under each of them. They rang softly in the dark, three slightly different notes, like a very sad little band.

She had carried Romeo the whole way inside the front of her robe, his long body folded against her ribs and his ears flopping over her sash, because she said a wet dog trotting at her heels would draw questions and a lump in her robe would only draw jokes. She was used to jokes.

Now she set him down on the floor, lit a stub of candle, sat on the edge of her sleeping platform, and stared at him.

Romeo stared back. He was good at this. He had once out-stared a Nascent Soul tiger for an entire afternoon over the last dumpling at a roadside stall.

"Say something," she said at last.

⟨Your roof leaks.⟩

She flinched, then laughed, a short, startled sound she seemed to regret. "So I didn't imagine it."

⟨You did not.⟩

"You're a talking dog."

⟨I am an awakened canine of the Qi Gathering realm, first level, formerly of the Tribulation Transcendence realm, sixty-ninth level. I am also, yes, a dog. The two are not in conflict.⟩

"Sixty-ninth." She said it slowly. "There are maybe three people in the whole first tier past the fortieth. The Sect Master is forty-four."

⟨Then your Sect Master is doing well for his age. His great-great-grandfather cried when he lost at Go.⟩

Ling Mei put her face in her hands. Her shoulders shook. Romeo could not tell whether it was laughter or the other thing until the smell reached him, salt and relief and a long, long tiredness, and he understood that it was both.

He waited. He had sat by a grave for nine years. He could sit by a girl for a few minutes.

---

When she had finished, she wiped her eyes on her mended sleeve and became practical, which he had already guessed was how she did everything.

"Right," she said. "That panel. The one that asked if you wanted to bind me. I saw it too, a little. What does that make me? Am I your master now?"

Romeo's hackles rose before he could stop them. The word did that to him. He thought of silver links whispering like coins, and of a calm voice saying *sit*, and of his own body folding under him like a well-trained pet's.

⟨No,⟩ he said. ⟨There is a difference between a binding and a bond. A binding is a chain. One end commands and the other obeys. That is what your sect does to its wolves. What we have is a bond. Neither end holds it. Either of us may walk away.⟩

"Then why bind at all?"

⟨Because my tail made the decision before I did,⟩ Romeo said with great dignity. ⟨We will not discuss it further.⟩

She pressed her lips together very hard.

⟨Terms,⟩ he went on. ⟨I will make you strong. Not sect-strong. Strong. In return you will hide me, feed me when you can, and never once call me "good boy" in front of other people.⟩

"And in private?"

Romeo considered this for slightly longer than a three-thousand-year-old cultivator should have.

⟨In private it will be tolerated.⟩

"Done." She held out her hand, then seemed to realize the problem, and turned it palm up. Romeo set one white forepaw in it. It was a small paw, and her hand was a calloused one, and for a moment the candle made a shadow on the wall of an old man's knobbly fingers holding the paw of a much younger dog.

The System, which had opinions about promises, unfolded a panel between them.

**\[QUEST ISSUED: Nine Days to Stop Being a Puppy\]**  
**\[Objective 1: Correct companion Ling Mei's meridian flow. (0/1)\]**  
**\[Objective 2: Survive the Hound-Catcher. (0/1)\]**  
**\[Time remaining: 8 days, 22 hours.\]**  
**\[Reward: Unknown.\]**

Ling Mei read it aloud under her breath. "Survive?"

⟨Details,⟩ said Romeo.

---

The first problem was the sleeping platform.

It stood at the height of Ling Mei's knee, which was to say about twice the height of Romeo's entire body, and it was the only dry surface in the hut. He walked up to it. He looked at it. He crouched, wiggled his hindquarters, and launched himself with every bit of his agility seven.

His front paws caught the edge. His back paws did not. For a long moment he hung there, legs paddling at the air, his long middle sagging like a wet rope between a cliff and nothing, while Ling Mei clapped both hands over her mouth.

⟨Do not,⟩ he said, ⟨help.⟩

She helped. She scooped him up under the chest and the hindquarters together, the way his kind should always be lifted, supporting the long back, and he noted with grudging approval that nobody had needed to tell her that.

⟨Your floor,⟩ he said, once he was settled on the thin blanket with what remained of his pride, ⟨is packed earth under those boards?⟩

"Yes, why?"

⟨No reason.⟩

There was every reason. Every instinct bred into his line over centuries of farmers sending small, stubborn dogs down holes after badgers was whispering to him: *earth. Den. Under.* A dog with a den is a dog with a back door. Tomorrow, he decided, he would dig.

Tonight he had a promise to keep.

⟨Lie down,⟩ he said. ⟨On your back. Hands at your sides. Breathe as your sect taught you.⟩

She obeyed, uncertain. Romeo walked up the length of her, which took him a few short-legged steps, and put his nose against the hollow of her throat. Then he closed his eyes and breathed in.

The fragmented Heavenly Nose woke slowly, like an old joint on a cold morning. Most cultivators read meridians with spiritual sense, a cold, clinical sweep. Romeo had never been able to do that. What he did was smell them. Qi moving correctly through a body smelled like water running downhill, clean and inevitable. Qi forced the wrong way smelled like a river being pushed back up a mountain: bruised, crowded, sour with effort.

Ling Mei's meridians smelled like a flood held behind a wall.

He followed the scent of it down her arms, along the twelve primary channels, into the sea of qi below her navel. Everything her sect manual told her to do, the Azure Wolf Breathing Method that every outer disciple chanted at dawn, pushed qi inward from the extremities toward the dantian. And everything in her body was built to pull it the other way. For four years she had been breathing against her own grain, every single day, and wondering why nothing happened.

Romeo lifted his head.

⟨I have smelled this twice before,⟩ he said. ⟨The first time was in a fisherman's daughter on the Southern Coast, two thousand years ago. The second was in a monk who later founded the Tidewater Temple. You have what the old texts call the Ebbtide Vessel. Your qi does not gather inward like a pond. It goes out and comes back like the sea. Your sect has been teaching a tide to stand still.⟩

"So I'm not crippled," she said. Very quietly. As if the word might break if she said it louder.

⟨You have been rowing upstream for four years, and you are still in the boat,⟩ Romeo said. ⟨That is the opposite of crippled.⟩

She didn't answer. He let the silence be.

---

Old Man Shen had taught him to breathe in a cave above the village, the winter Romeo was a year old and the snow came up to the old man's knees, which meant it came well over Romeo's head. The old man had not known anything about qi. He had known about herbs, and about dogs, and about how to sit still in the cold.

"Don't chase the air," he used to say, rubbing Romeo's ears with fingers stained green from bitterroot. "Let it come to your nose. The good smells always find you if you wait."

Romeo had built a cultivation method on that sentence. It had taken him three hundred years. He had never told anyone where it came from.

---

⟨Forget the Azure Wolf Method,⟩ he told Ling Mei. ⟨Breathe out first. All the way, until your belly is empty. Then do not pull the breath in. Let it come back on its own, like a wave coming up a beach. Follow it with your attention, but do not push.⟩

"That's it?"

⟨That is the beginning. The middle and end are harder. Breathe.⟩

She breathed. Out, long and slow. A pause. The breath came back.

The first few times, nothing. Her face tightened with the old frustration and Romeo smelled it rising, sharp and sour, the habit of four years of failure. He pressed his cold nose firmly against her wrist.

⟨Stop trying,⟩ he said. ⟨You are trying exactly the way you were taught, which is the wrong way. Be lazy. Be a dog in a patch of sun.⟩

"I don't know how to be a dog in a patch of sun."

⟨Clearly. I will teach you that as well. Breathe.⟩

The candle burned down a finger's width. The three bowls rang under the leaks. And somewhere around the fortieth breath, Romeo smelled it: the sour, crowded scent of pushed-back water loosening. Thinning. Turning, very slightly, very slowly, like a tide on the point of changing.

Ling Mei gasped. Her fingers twitched against the blanket.

"Something moved," she whispered. "In my hands. Something warm went *out*, and then it came back, and it was more."

⟨Yes,⟩ said Romeo, and found that his tail was wagging again, and this time did not bother to argue with it.

**\[Companion Ling Mei has sensed qi for the first time.\]**  
**\[Quest progress: Objective 1 — meridian flow 1% corrected.\]**

"One percent," Ling Mei said, reading it. She laughed again, the startled kind. "One percent\!"

⟨One percent more than in four years. Do not let it go to your head. We will do this every night until the tide turns properly. It will hurt, later. Reversing a four-year habit always does.⟩

She was already breathing again, eyes closed, a small fierce smile on her face. Romeo watched her for a while. Then, because he had his own work, he curled into a tight crescent at her hip, nose to tail, and began the first layer of the Nine Scents Heavenly Tracking Sutra.

The night air came in through the torn window screen, and he let it come to him. Wet bamboo. Woodsmoke. Wolf musk from the Beast Hall on the hill. Faint threads of heaven-and-earth qi riding the scents like dust in a sunbeam, which he gathered one by one and drew down into his own small, empty sea. It was slow. It was pitifully slow. At the peak of his power, a single breath had drawn in more qi than this hut held in a year.

But it was a beginning, and he had started from nothing once before.

---

He woke at dawn with his head on Ling Mei's ankle and someone hammering on the door.

Romeo was off the platform before he had decided to move, landing in an undignified heap and scrambling under the platform's shadow with his belly flat to the boards. Ling Mei sat up, hair flattened on one side, and opened the door a crack.

It was a junior steward Romeo did not know, a young man with ink-stained fingers holding a stack of thin bamboo slips. He looked at Ling Mei the way people look at a chore.

"Notice for all outer disciples," he said, and held out a slip. "By order of Elder Fang Wuji. Any outer disciple keeping an animal on sect grounds, spirit beast or otherwise, is to present it at the Beast Hall for registration and assessment at the hour of the snake tomorrow. Unregistered beasts found after that will be seized."

Ling Mei did not take the slip. "Why would that matter to me?"

The steward shrugged. "Couldn't say, Senior Sister. Only, Disciple Zhao Peng told the discipline hall you've got a little red dog that steals pills off people's belts." He pushed the slip into her hand. "The Elder seemed very interested."

The door closed. His footsteps squelched away down the ditch.

Under the sleeping platform, in the dark, Romeo breathed in the faint scent the steward had left behind on the bamboo slip. Ink. Rain. And beneath them both, on the slip itself, where an elder's fingers had pressed the order seal: wolf musk, old and heavy, and something colder underneath it.

Something that smelled, very faintly, like silver.
