# **Chapter 17: What the Stone Keeps**

The stone came out at the hour of the snake, on a sledge, hauled by four outer disciples who had plainly drawn the short straws.

It was smaller than Romeo had expected. From the way the sect talked about it he had imagined something the size of a gate. It was waist-high to a man, rough and grey and roughly the shape of a tooth, with a band of silver worked round its middle like a collar. The four disciples set it down in the cleared centre of the yard, before the reception-hall steps, and backed away from it wiping their hands on their robes. Romeo did not blame them. Even from the end of Ling Mei's line, swaddled to the ears, he could smell it.

Old rock. Third-tier rock, the high cold kind that never quite thaws. And under the rock, so faint it was nearly a taste, a great many small sweet notes crowded together, like a jar of honey-sweets that someone had kept stealing from and never eaten.

*Names,* he thought. *It's full of names.*

⟨Ling Mei,⟩ he said into her mind. ⟨Whatever happens, don't put me down near that thing until they make you.⟩

"I wasn't planning to put you down anywhere," she murmured, without moving her lips, which she had learned to do in about two days and which still impressed him.

---

The collector did not begin with the wolves. He began with the cage.

At a flick of his grey glove, the two broad escorts lifted it down from the riderless horse and set it beside the stone, and one of them drew off the oiled cloth. The yard made a sound. It was not a gasp; outer disciples were too cold and too careful to gasp. It was the shuffling, half-swallowed noise of two hundred people recognising something at once.

The fox lay curled in the straw. She was smaller than Brow's memory of her, or perhaps only thinner. Her tail, which must once have been the bright thing her name promised, was wrapped round her nose. Her eyes were open. They did not move.

"Some of you will remember her," said the collector pleasantly. "She stood just here a month ago, and the stone was very taken with her. Afterwards she was sold. The paperwork came through my office, as such things do." He bent, opened the cage door, and one of the escorts reached in and lifted her out by the scruff and set her on the snow before the stone. She did not struggle. She sat where she was put. "A demonstration, Elder Fang, for the benefit of your disciples. So they know what they are looking at."

The silver band on the stone stayed dark.

"Nothing," said the collector. "You see? That is what a finished beast looks like. Quiet. Easy to hold. Nothing left in her for anything to take hold of except the leash." He nodded, and the escort put her back in the cage, and nobody covered it again.

On the steps, Fang Wuji's hand had come down onto the ruff of Brow's neck. Romeo smelled the binding between them pull tight, a long cold note drawn suddenly thin. He could not tell which end was doing the pulling. He was not sure Fang could either.

---

Then the wolves.

They came one at a time, each with its handler, and each handler called the beast's name in front of the stone, and each time the silver band answered. The light was pale and pretty, like frost catching a lamp, and the name hung in it a breath before fading: *Iron-Jaw. Third Cloud. Ashfoot.* The disciples murmured approval. A deacon wrote. It was all very orderly, and Romeo, who had stood at the edges of a great many orderly ceremonies in three thousand years, had learned to distrust nothing quite so much.

So he did what he was for. He shut his eyes, let his head hang on Ling Mei's arm like a sick dog's, and put his whole nose on it.

Grey came up seventh. Grey Nine, lean and scarred, who had called Romeo "hole-dog" on his second day and been right. His handler called, the band lit, *Grey Nine*, and the wolf turned away as all the others had. As he passed the end of Ling Mei's line, Romeo breathed him in.

Fur. Kennel straw. Silver. And under it, at the very bottom, the deep note that was the wolf's name, the place he turned toward. It was there. It was also thinner than it had been on the second day, like a well that someone had let down a bucket into and drawn up full.

Romeo opened his eyes. He sniffed Ashfoot, two places along, and Iron-Jaw beyond. The same. Every wolf that had stood before the stone came away with a little less of itself at the bottom of its smell.

**\[Heavenly Nose: insight gained.\]**  
**\[Bound beasts assessed: 7\. Name-scent diminished: 7 of 7.\]**  
**\[The stone does not only read.\]**

*It keeps,* thought Romeo, and his skin went cold under the wet fur in a way that had nothing to do with snow. *Every year they bring them here and call them, and it takes a sip. And a fox who gives it everything all at once...*

He did not finish the thought. He did not need to. She was sitting in a cage ten paces from the stone, looking at nothing.

---

The beast-physician brought Smoke up in the middle of the wolves, and that was very nearly the end of everything.

It was not the stoat's fault. The stoat was only being a stoat: long, low, quick, a ripple of brown down the old man's sleeve and onto the snow, all bright eyes and busy nose. But Romeo was a dachshund, and three thousand years of cultivation lay over that fact like a fine silk robe over a badger. Something very old and very simple woke up in his chest and said, quite clearly, *That. Into the ground after that. Now.*

His back legs bunched inside the blanket. His ears came up. A sound began in the bottom of his throat that was not a growl yet but had every intention of becoming one.

Ling Mei's good hand closed round his muzzle, gently and absolutely. "No," she said aloud, in the voice of every exasperated dog-owner in the world, and two disciples near her snickered.

⟨I wasn't going to,⟩ said Romeo, with enormous dignity, through her fingers.

⟨You were,⟩ she thought back.

⟨I was considering it in an abstract way.⟩

The physician, at the stone, did not look round. But when the band lit for the stoat (*Smoke*, pale and brief), the old man laid two fingers flat on the grey rock for a moment, the way a man lays fingers on a sick beast's flank to feel for a fever, and took them away again, and said nothing to anyone.

---

Fang Wuji called Brow himself.

He did not come down the steps to do it. He said the name from where he stood, quietly, the way a man calls a beast he has called ten thousand times. "Brow." And the great wolf went down the steps and across the yard and stood before the stone, and did not look at the open cage beside it, and did not look at the fox inside it, and that not-looking was the loudest thing Romeo had smelled all morning.

The band lit. *Brow*, it said, plain and strong.

And under it, for less than a heartbeat, something else. A cold white flicker, like a gust of snow across a lantern.

The mouthless mask turned toward it. Romeo felt the whole yard not notice, and the collector notice.

"He's old," said Fang Wuji, before anyone could ask. "Old beasts collect cobwebs. Come, Brow."

The wolf came. He climbed the steps and lay down at the elder's knee. As he went by, Romeo breathed him in with his whole body, and there it was, at the very bottom, under the silver and the straw: a name with snow in it. Thinner, perhaps. Still there. Brow had stood in front of the thing that ate names and kept his own, and kept a fox's too, and given the stone nothing but the one his master used.

*Good dog,* thought Romeo, and meant it with every one of his three thousand years.

---

He was last. He had known he would be. Sick dogs always are.

Ling Mei carried him out of the line and across the trampled snow, and the yard watched them come, the girl they called Rank Zero and the wet sausage of a dog in her blanket. She unwrapped him. The cold hit his damp coat like a slap. She set him down before the stone, and from down there it was no longer waist-high. It was a cliff. The silver band ran round it at the height of a wolf's head, a long way above his own.

At his throat, the shaving stirred and leaned, very gently, toward the fox's cage.

**\[QUEST COMPLETE: Anything Less Than the End of the World\]**  
**\[Final condition met: you returned before the naming.\]**  
**\[Reward already received. Bindings severed: 3 of 27.\]**  
**\[Next condition: none. You are on your own.\]**

*Thank you,* thought Romeo. *Very encouraging.*

Ling Mei drew breath to call him.

"Inspector." It was Zhao Peng's uncle, stepping down from among the deacons with his soft hands folded and his good silk shining. He smelled of pork fat, and of the thing he had been looking forward to, arriving. "Forgive me. Only it occurs to me: any dog will come to the hand that feeds it. If the girl calls, we learn what the dog thinks of the girl. If we wish to know what the beast truly is..." He spread his hands. "Surely someone it doesn't love should call it."

"Yes," said the collector. "I'd been thinking the same."

Ling Mei did not move. "He won't come to a stranger, Inspector. He's a dog."

"Step back, disciple," said Fang Wuji.

For one long breath she did not. Romeo smelled it in her, the fear and the fury and the decision, and he turned his head and looked up at her, and he did not need mind-speech for it. *Go on. Three steps. I'm the correct length for this.*

She took three steps back. Exactly three. Her right hand was shaking. Her left would not straighten.

The collector came across the snow and crouched before the stone, so that the pale lacquer mask was nearly at Romeo's level. Up close it smelled of lacquer, and horse, and frost. He drew off one grey glove, finger by finger, and held out a bare hand, palm down, the way you offer a hand to a strange dog. Romeo smelled it before he could stop himself.

Clean skin. Silver, a great deal of it. And under the silver, very faint, so faint it might have been nothing but the inside of a riding glove, a trace of two other things.

Silk. And lamp smoke.

Romeo's nose went still. Somewhere on the mountain behind him, a tall narrow thing that did not sweat was walking toward a lake.

"Romeo," said the collector.

He did not say it the way the ledger had. He did not say it the way anyone in these hills said anything. He said it with the first sound long and the last one lifting, soft, unhurried, like a pebble dropped into slow water. It was the way the name had been said on a riverbank in a valley that no longer had a name, all one golden afternoon, by an old man with his trouser legs rolled up.

No one had said it like that in three thousand years.

Romeo's tail moved. He did not know which way it was going to go.

Above him, slowly, the silver band began to glow, and for the first time in three thousand one hundred and twelve years, he did not know which name it was going to say.
