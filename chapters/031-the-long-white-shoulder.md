# **Chapter 31: The Long White Shoulder**

Romeo lay with his chin on Ling Mei's arm and did not say anything for a long time, because everything he could think of to say was the kind of thing people say at gravesides, and Brow was not dead.

⟨What does it feel like?⟩ he asked at last.

The wolf took his time. Far up the hill, in a warm room above a cold yard, a great blue-white animal was lying beside a sleeping man and choosing his words.

⟨Like being picked up by the scruff,⟩ said Brow. ⟨When you are very small, and your mother carries you. You go limp. You cannot help it.⟩ A pause. ⟨I am still limp, small dog. Something has hold of the back of my neck, and it goes up the hill.⟩

*Leaning,* thought Romeo. *Like the others. Like Ah-Shu's palm.* He had smelled it on Grey Nine and Iron-Jaw for days: name-scent thinning, tilting uphill like grass in a slow current. Brow had always smelled square and solid as a stone wall. Romeo could not smell him from here. He did not need to.

⟨Stay away from the stone,⟩ said Romeo.

⟨I am to be in his room on the dark night. He has seen to it.⟩ Something rusty moved in the wolf's voice that might, in a younger animal, have been a laugh. ⟨He thinks of everything except me.⟩

⟨He should be told.⟩

⟨No.⟩ The word came down the hill like a dropped stone. ⟨If you tell him, he will do something brave. He has paid enough for me. Ask your turtle, small dog. Then come and tell me what I am.⟩

After that there was only the wind.

---

Ling Mei woke before light, heard it all from him with her eyes shut, and lay still for twenty breaths.

"Right," she said, and got up, and went to draw buckets.

She drew nine before noon, which the junior steward wrote down without comment, and then two more because he looked at her, and then she came back to the hut with something wrapped in a rag inside her sleeve.

It was a bun. A steamed bun, grey-white and slightly squashed, from the outer kitchen, where the cook gave away the morning's broken ones to whoever was standing nearest when he lost his temper.

She sat on the third floorboard and tore it in half.

Romeo looked at the half in front of him for longer than a dog should look at food. The last time a girl had torn a bun in half for him, it had just been knocked into the mud by a boy with an uncle, and it had been all she had, and she had given him the bigger piece.

This was also the bigger piece.

⟨You know what happened last time,⟩ he said.

"I got a lodger," said Ling Mei. "Eat it before I change my mind."

He ate it. He did not tilt his head up at her afterward with his eyes very wide, in the manner of a dog explaining that there had been some mistake and the bun was gone. He considered it, and did not, and his tail swept the floorboard twice on its own account.

"I saw that," said Ling Mei.

⟨It's a draught.⟩

---

They went at dusk, the way he went everywhere now: out through the back-door tunnel on his belly, forty level paces, to the ditch reeds where she was waiting with her padded coat open. North between the last two storehouses, where no lamp reached. Through the black pines, past the spring that steamed in the cold, without stopping. Romeo kept his nose shut against the silver smell of the seam. He did not want to know, tonight, how far up the hill Brow's name had gone.

Then the trees fell away below them and there was only the shoulder.

He had forgotten how big it was. He had crossed it once on his own feet, under it more than over it, burrowing along the warm line of the seam, and it had nearly finished him. He had crossed it once in this coat, and slept through the worst of it. Awake, from inside Ling Mei's coat, with his front paws over her collar and her crooked left arm braced under his hindquarters, it looked bigger. A long white slope going up and over into nothing, smooth as a sheet laid over a sleeping giant, with the wind lifting snow off the top of it in a pale continual smoke.

"Same way as last time?" said Ling Mei. "I only remember the coming back. In daylight."

⟨Up,⟩ said Romeo. ⟨Then over. The wind's filled in your old trail.⟩ He sniffed. ⟨Then down. The lake smells like cold iron and very old mud. When you can smell it, I'll stop being useful and start being luggage.⟩

"You're always luggage," said Ling Mei, and started up.

She was not a strong walker. Her left arm would not straighten and her right hand shook, and in deep snow the shaking went up into her shoulder and made her stagger. But she was stubborn in the way that water is stubborn, which is to say she did not argue with the slope; she simply kept arriving at the next part of it. Romeo rode against her chest with his nose out in the wind and steered.

⟨Left. Bit more. There's rock under there; it'll hold you.⟩

"How do you know?"

⟨It smells of lichen. Snow over grass smells of nothing.⟩

"Everything smells of nothing," said Ling Mei. "It's snow."

⟨To you,⟩ said Romeo kindly, and she bumped him with her chin.

---

Halfway up, the wind changed.

Romeo smelled it before he felt it: a sharpening in the air, the iron edge going out of everything and something blunter and wetter coming in from the north. He had been caught on high ground in weather like that more times than he could remember, in more bodies than he cared to count. He knew exactly what it meant.

⟨Ling Mei. Stop.⟩

"We're nearly at the top."

⟨We are not nearly at anything. Look at the ridge.⟩

She looked. The pale smoke coming off the crest had turned grey and thick and was no longer going up. It was coming down towards them, very fast, filling the slope like milk poured into a bowl.

The first gust knocked her to her knees. The second put her on her side in the snow, and Romeo spilled half out of the coat, and for a moment there was nothing in the world but white and roaring and the taste of ice.

He found her by smell. Ink, well rope, wet wool, fear. She had her right arm up over her face and was trying to get up and the wind was not letting her.

*Shelter,* thought Romeo. *Lee. Rock. Hole.*

The old part of him, the part that was three thousand years old and had commanded rivers, thought about it. The older part of him, the part that was a small, long, low dog bred by patient people for one job, did not think about anything at all. It simply turned its nose to the slope and found, a body's length away, the hump of a boulder buried to its shoulders, and the lee side where the snow had packed into a hard sloping drift.

He went in.

Snow was not earth. It came away too easily and fell back too fast, and it was so cold that his paws stopped feeling it after the first dozen strokes. But a dachshund does not need to feel his paws to dig. He needs a direction and a grudge. Romeo put his chest against the drift and threw snow out between his hind legs in two white fans, the way his kind had thrown dirt out of badger setts for longer than there had been sects, and the wind took it and flung it away, and he went in further.

His back spoke up at once. Then it spoke up louder. He gave it nothing to argue with: front paws, chest, shoulders, never a twist, the long spine kept straight and level as a plank. *Stop trying to be tall,* said Old Man Shen somewhere, a very long time ago, and Romeo, in a snowdrift on a mountain, did not.

*I know,* thought Romeo. *Go away.*

It was not much of a hole. A hollow under the boulder's lip, as long as a girl curled up and as high as a girl's shoulder when she was lying on her side, with the rock for a roof and the packed drift for walls. He backed out of it and bit Ling Mei's sleeve and pulled.

"What—"

⟨In. Now. Don't argue with me, I'm a professional.⟩

She crawled in on her elbows. He came in after her and lay across the mouth of the hole, which was where a dachshund lies, and let the snow begin to drift over his back and seal them in.

---

The roaring went on outside. Inside, it was very dark and very quiet, and almost at once it was warmer, the way a burrow is always warmer than the world it is dug into.

Ling Mei was shaking, not the tremor in her hand but the whole-body shaking of someone too cold to stop. Romeo crawled up beside her, under her chin, his long back against her ribs, the way they had lain on the floor last night.

Then he did something he had not done before.

He did not push his qi into her, as he had in Fang's room. He did not pull it back, as he had on the floorboard. He let a little of it go down the bond on purpose, a thread, no more, and he followed it. He knew the road now. Down when it wanted to go up. In when it wanted to go out. He walked it through her the wrong way round until it came back to him, and where it had passed, she was warm.

He did it again. And again. A thread at a time, round and back, round and back, like a dog turning in its basket before it lies down. It cost him very little; what went out came home, and it came home a little denser every time, like river stone.

Ling Mei stopped shaking.

"You're doing it again," she said, into the dark. "The soup."

⟨Just a sip.⟩

"It's better when you ask." She was quiet a moment. "You knew how to do that. The hole."

⟨I am a very old dog.⟩

"No. Not that." Her chin moved against his head. "Not the old part. The dog part. You didn't think. You just went."

⟨Yes,⟩ said Romeo. He did not like admitting it. He admitted it. ⟨Sometimes the dog part knows better.⟩

"Shen said that, didn't he."

⟨No,⟩ said Romeo. ⟨Shen would have said I was showing off.⟩

She laughed, a small cracked sound, and he felt her fall asleep. He did not. He lay with his nose in the gap at the mouth of the hole and breathed the storm, and when the iron edge came back into the air, sometime after midnight, he woke her.

---

The world on the other side of the storm was new. The slope lay smooth and blank and deeper than before, glittering under stars that seemed close enough to bite, and the wind had gone down to almost nothing.

They went over the top together.

On the far side the snow was waist-deep on her and over Romeo's head, and she could not carry him all the way down; her arms had nothing left. So she went first, breaking the trail, and Romeo came after her on his own short legs, putting his feet in her footprints, one, and then the next one.

She looked back once and saw him doing it, and did not laugh. He was grateful for that. He was also, slightly, disappointed.

Below them, at the bottom of the long white fall, the lake lay under its ice like something sleeping under a sheet, and the air smelled of cold iron and very old mud.

---

The sky was going grey in the east when they reached the ice. Romeo went out onto it alone, belly low, ears trailing, to the place above the deep water where he had knocked before. He set his chest to the ice and listened. Far below, something huge and slow lay in the mud, breathing once in a long while, the way mountains breathe.

He knocked. Three, with his paw. Then two.

For a long time nothing happened. Ling Mei stood at the edge of the ice with her arms wrapped round herself, the only upright thing in all that white.

Then, through the ice and through his chest and up into his skull, came the old slow voice, slower than he had ever heard it, and very, very quiet.

⟨Little brother,⟩ said Bao. ⟨Don't turn round. Tell me something.⟩

⟨What?⟩

⟨The girl at the edge of the ice. Who is that standing behind her?⟩

Romeo lifted his nose and breathed in the whole shore.

Snow. Iron. Old mud. Wet wool, ink, well rope, Ling Mei.

Nothing else at all.
