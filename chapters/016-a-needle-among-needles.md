# **Chapter 16: A Needle Among Needles**

Three li was nothing. Three li was the distance a horse covers while a dachshund is still deciding which way to face in a snowdrift.  
The shaving at Romeo's throat would not lie still. It pressed against the inside of the blue cloth like a small warm fingertip, pointing east, and every time the wind came down off the pass it seemed to lean a little harder, the way a puppy leans toward a door it has heard a familiar step behind. Romeo had spent three thousand years learning to keep his own body quiet. He had never before had to keep quiet a thing that was not his body and did not care what he wanted.  
*It knows its own,* he thought. *Wonderful. And its own is riding toward me with a cage.*  
On the ridge, the masked rider had lowered his face again. The line of horses kept coming, unhurried, down the long switchbacks of the pass. Romeo could not tell whether the man had caught anything or had only been smelling the weather. That was the trouble with masks. They kept the face in, and a great deal of the scent in with it.  
He looked back at the hut. Twelve paces of chest-deep snow, every one of which he had already ploughed, so that there was now a trench leading from Ling Mei's door to the exact spot where he stood, as plain as a line drawn in ink. Anybody with eyes could follow it. Anybody with a nose could follow the shaving.  
*Brow,* he said. *How loud am I?*  
The wolf considered him, pale-eyed, steaming gently. *To me? Like a cricket in a wall.* A pause. *To him, I do not know. He carries a great deal of silver. Things that carry a great deal of silver hear it well.*  
Romeo looked at the wolf. He looked, in particular, at the wolf's chest, which was deep as a barrel, and at the long pale legs under it, and at the space between those legs and the snow, which was exactly, insultingly, the height of a dachshund.  
Every bound beast in this sect smelled of silver. Brow smelled of it most of all. It was worked into his coat and his breath and the long cold line of the binding that ran from him up the mountain to Fang Wuji's hand. Beside that, a rice-grain curl at a small dog's throat was a cricket. Beneath it, perhaps, it was nothing at all.  
*Where would you hide a needle,* said Romeo, *if someone were looking for a needle?*  
Brow's ears tipped forward.  
*Stand up,* said Romeo. *And walk slowly. And for the love of every heaven, do not sit down.*  
---

It was not dignified. Romeo had stopped expecting dignity somewhere around the second day of being a puppy again, but even so, there were limits, and walking under a wolf was very near one of them.  
Brow rose out of the snow in one long unfolding, and the world above Romeo became fur. Blue-white fur, thick and frost-tipped, hanging down in a ceiling that brushed the top of his head and the tips of his ears. It smelled of straw and old kennels and silver, silver, silver, a great dull roar of it so close that his teeth ached. He pressed himself in under the wolf's ribs, matched the slow swing of the great forelegs on either side of him, and walked.  
His legs were much shorter than Brow's. This meant that for every step the wolf took, Romeo took three, scuttling, his belly furrowing the snow, his ears dragging, his tail held stiff and low behind him so as not to tangle in a hind leg. Twice Brow's foot came down a hair's breadth from his tail. Once the wolf, misjudging, slowed when Romeo did not, and Romeo walked his nose straight into the inside of a foreleg, and sneezed, and nearly lost the whole thing to a fit of indignation.  
*You are doing this on purpose,* he said.  
*I have never walked with a dog under me,* said Brow, with what might, in a less solemn creature, have been amusement. *You are very long.*  
*I am the correct length. Everything else is wrong.*  
But at his throat, the warm pull of the shaving faded. It did not stop. It went soft, the way a voice goes soft in a crowded room, one small note lost in the great dull chord of the wolf's silver. Romeo felt it waver, turn uncertainly, and point, of all things, up at Brow's belly, as though it had decided that this enormous animal was the nearest thing to home.

*You see?* said Romeo to nobody in particular. *A dog can be useful.*  
They went back along the trench, wolf and the dog under the wolf, as slowly as a funeral, and at the hut door Brow lay down across the threshold exactly as he had the night before, chin on paws, back to the world. Romeo slid out from under his chest and through the crack of the door like a sausage down a drain.  
Behind him, he heard the wolf breathe out, long and slow, and felt the faintest tremor go up the binding toward the elder's hall. Not a lie, this time. Romeo did not know what it was. He had a nose for lies, not for the other things a beast might say to the hand that held it.  
---

Ling Mei woke to a cold wet nose in her ear and sat up so fast she hit her head on the wall.  
"What, what—"  
*He's here,* said Romeo. *Three horses. Coming down the pass now. You have time to put your hair up and look surprised, and not much else.*  
She did not waste breath. She put her hair up. She brushed the snow off him with the corner of the blanket and found he was soaked again and said a word that outer disciples were not supposed to know. Then the bell on the lower terrace began to ring, the long double stroke that meant every outer disciple to the yard, and she picked him up, wrapped him in the blanket so that only his head and ears showed, and went out past Brow into the morning.  
The whole outer sect was there, or near enough. Two hundred disciples in grey and blue, breath smoking, shuffling in their lines. On the steps of the reception hall stood the deacons, and above them Elder Fang Wuji, with his hands in his sleeves and his face like a closed door. Romeo smelled Zhao Peng's uncle among the deacons before he saw him, a soft round man in good silk who smelled of pork fat and of looking forward to something. Zhao Peng himself was nowhere. Confined, still. The uncle's eyes went once across the yard, found Ling Mei, found the dog in her arms, and stayed there a moment too long.  
Then the gate opened, and the collector rode in.  
He was smaller than Romeo had expected. That was the first thing. A slight man in a long grey riding coat, sitting his horse very straight, with a mask of plain pale lacquer over his whole face, no mouth on it, only two narrow slits for eyes. Loops of silver chain hung from his saddle on both sides, fine and heavy and gleaming with frost, and they did not clink when the horse moved. Romeo did not like that at all. Chain that did not clink had been told not to.  
Behind him came two more riders, broad, masked in the same pale lacquer, and behind them a third horse with no rider at all, carrying a cage.  
The cage was covered with oiled cloth. Cage-bar iron, the smell said, and straw, and under the straw something alive.  
The collector dismounted. He did not bow to the elder. Fang Wuji did not bow to him. They regarded one another across the width of the steps, and the whole yard went quiet in the particular way a yard goes quiet when two large dogs meet at a gate.  
"Elder Fang." The voice came out of the mouthless mask light and pleasant and very clear. "You'll forgive me for being early. I had word there was a small matter in these hills worth hurrying for."  
"The Azure Wolf Sect is always honoured," said Fang Wuji, in the voice of a man who meant the exact opposite. "Every beast in my keeping is registered. Every one is bound. You'll find nothing in my ledger to hurry for."  
"Then I'll be pleased to read it." A hand in a grey glove held out. A deacon, sweating, hurried down with the beast ledger and put it in it.  
The collector read standing in the snow. He read slowly, one page and then the next, and the yard waited. Romeo, in Ling Mei's arms, did the hardest thing a dog can do, which is nothing. He let his head loll. He let his ears hang. He let his nose run, which it was doing anyway. He was a sick wet dog in a blanket, and he was being bored by a long ceremony, and he would really, at this point, like his breakfast.  
The gloved finger stopped.  
"'One dog,'" read the collector. "'Unawakened. Name: Romeo. Bound to outer disciple Ling Mei.'" The slits of the mask lifted and moved, unhurried, along the lines of disciples, and found her. "Romeo," he said again, as though trying the weight of it. "That's an old name to give a dog in these hills."  
Ling Mei's arms went tight around him. Romeo smelled her fear come up and smelled her shove it back down, the way she shoved everything, with one good hand.  
"It's the one he comes to, Inspector," she said.  
And at the sound of it, in front of two hundred disciples and a Nascent Soul elder and a man in a mouthless mask, Romeo's tail thumped twice against her ribs through the blanket, without asking anyone.  
Somebody in the third row laughed. Then several people did. It was the laughter of a cold yard at a stupid dog, and Romeo had never loved a sound more.  
The mask watched him for one breath longer. Then the collector closed the ledger.  
"Charming," he said. "We'll see what the stone thinks of it."  
---

It was only when Ling Mei had carried him back to the end of her line, and the collector had turned away to speak with the elder, and Brow had come padding down the terrace steps to take his place at Fang Wuji's knee, that the shaving woke again.  
Without the wolf's great chord over it, the little note came back at once, warm and urgent at Romeo's throat. But it was not pointing at the collector. Romeo felt it swing past the grey coat, past the riders and their soundless chains, and settle, trembling, on the third horse. On the covered cage.  
*It knows its own,* he thought. *So what in there is its own?*  
The wind turned. It came across the yard from the gate, and it brought the cage with it, under the oiled cloth: straw, iron, old fear, and something else, faint and green and impossible.  
Bamboo.  
Romeo's nose went still. Under the bamboo was fox. A small vixen, young, frightened past the point of shaking. He knew her the way he knew every scent he had ever been told about and never smelled, because Brow had carried her in his mind like a thing wrapped up in cloth, and some of it had come across when they touched noses in the snow. Bright-Tail-Under-the-Bamboo. Sold to the merchant. Gone.  
Not gone. Here, in a cage, on a horse, in the yard where she had last been happy.  
And the scent of her was wrong. Every beast has a name in its smell. It is the deepest note of all, the one under fur and fear and supper, the place a creature turns toward when it is called. Romeo had never once in three thousand years smelled a living thing without one.  
He smelled one now.

On the steps, Brow's head came up. The great wolf turned, slowly, toward the gate and the third horse and the covered cage, and every hair along his back rose at once, exactly as it had at dawn.  
Under the oiled cloth, something small lifted its head to the smell of him, and made no sound at all.
