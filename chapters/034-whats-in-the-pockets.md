# **Chapter 34: What's in the Pockets**

The second paw went before he could stop it.

Right forepaw, this time. It lifted, and reached, and came down on the ice a step nearer the shore, as neatly as a dog stepping out of a doorway when somebody says *walk*. His shoulders followed it. His long back, which had never once in three thousand years done anything without complaining first, began to follow his shoulders without a word.

*No,* thought Romeo.

It was not a thought that had any qi in it. It was older than qi. It was the thought of every dachshund who has ever been asked to leave a warm place for a cold one, and it went down through him from the top of his skull to the white tip of his tail, and what it said was: *I will not.*

He went flat.

Not down. Flat. All four short legs went out sideways at once like the legs of a dropped stool, his chest hit the ice, his belly hit the ice, his chin hit the ice, and his long ears spread on either side of his head like two gloves somebody had lost. There is no weight in the world like a small dog who has decided not to go for a walk. Emperors had learned this. A Nascent Soul cultivator in a red robe had once tried to lift him off a temple step by the scruff and had gone home with sore wrists and a story nobody believed.

The thread pulled.

It did not pull hard. That was the worst of it. It pulled the way a patient hand pulls, steady and kind, as though it had all morning and knew he would come round. And it did not pull on his legs or his neck or the harness round his ribs. It pulled on something behind his breastbone that was shaped like a word.

*Romeo,* it said, without saying anything. *Come on, then.*

His claws were short and black and very good for digging. He drove them into the ice. They skittered, caught, skittered, and caught again in the old scratches of the morning, and he held there, shaking, with the whole lake under his chest and the whole of the mountain in front of him and something on the far end of his own name humming him toward the shore.

**\[Registered name "Romeo": called along binding.\]**  
**\[Resistance: partial. Host stubbornness: noted.\]**

*Noted,* thought Romeo. *Write it bigger.*

Behind him, Ling Mei turned over in her sleep.

He felt her do it. Her knees came up, the coat rustled, and her good hand, the right one, the one that still had a tremor in it after buckets, came out from under her cheek and found the loose skin at the back of his neck and closed on it. She did not wake. She did not pull. She simply held on, the way she had held on through the whole cold flood of Bao's qi: not tight, the way you hold a dog in a crowd.

The thread went on pulling.

The hand went on holding.

And Romeo lay between them, flat as a draught-stopper, with his ears out and his claws in, and discovered that it was easier to refuse something when someone was holding your scruff who would never once ask you to come.

The pull stopped.

It did not slacken by degrees. It simply ceased, the way a fisherman's fingers stop on a line once he has learned what he wanted to learn. The thread lay humming under the ice toward the shore and the slot and the passage. Romeo's paws were his again.

**\[Call ended.\]**  
**\[Holder: unnamed. Holder: patient.\]**

He lay there a long time with his chin on the ice and his heart going like a sparrow's, and he thought: *It wasn't trying. It was finding out how heavy I am.*

---

The day came up white and stayed white.

Ling Mei slept on Bao's grey patch with her mouth a little open and her hand still in his scruff, and Romeo did not sleep. He could not have if he had wanted to. Every channel he had was humming with the morning's two levels the way a bell goes on humming after it has been struck, and under the hum was the thread, and he did not intend to close his eyes on either of them.

So he watched the shore.

There was nothing on it. Snow, and the black rocks at the edge, and the dark place up the slope where the slot went into the mountain. No hole in the wind; he checked, nose up, every time the breeze came round. Under him the great slow breathing of the turtle had gone so far down that he had stopped being able to find it with his chest, and had to take it on trust.

*It knows how heavy I am now,* he thought. *On the dark night it won't find out. It will just pull.*

He made himself think it through, the way Ling Mei thought things through, slowly and all the way to the end.

A held promise does exactly what was promised and not one thing more, Bao had said. Ah-Shu's hand would bring a little dog up a passage because a man had said yes at a door. That was a lead with a hand on it. This was a lead with a name on it. Ah-Shu's hand could be tucked in a belt, or broken at the fingers by a man who had said he would. A name could not be tucked anywhere. It was behind his breastbone, and it was tied to his oldest friend's back left foot, and something at the other end of it could walk him like a goose to market.

Unless he bit it. In which case he would still be Romeo, and would not know why.

He thought about what he would lose.

---

The river had been brown that year, high with snowmelt off the second tier, and loud.

Shen had been crossing at the stepping stones below the willows with a basket of cloud-ear on his back and his digging stick in his hand, the way he always crossed, with his bad knee held a little stiff and his feet turned out. Halfway over, the wind had come down the valley like a door slamming, and the hat had gone off his head.

Romeo remembered it turning in the air. Straw, wide, red-mended at the brim. It had landed on the water brim-up, like a boat, and spun once, and gone.

He remembered Shen shouting *don't.*

He remembered not hearing it. That was the important part. Not refusing it. Not hearing it at all, because there was the hat, and it was the old man's, and it was leaving, and a young dog with too much body and not enough leg had already gone off the stone with his ears inside out before any part of him that could listen had been consulted.

The water had been so cold it was loud. It had rolled him. It had put the bank where the sky should be and the sky where the riverbed should be and filled his long nose with brown. He had kept his jaws shut on the brim the whole way down because they were shut on it and that was what jaws did. Half a li downstream an eddy had spat him into the reeds, and he had crawled out on his short legs with the hat in his mouth and the river in his lungs and been sick on Shen's feet.

Shen had sat down in the mud.

He had been laughing and coughing at once, wet to the elbows from wading after him, his face grey with fright and creased with laughing, and he had taken the hat out of the dog's mouth and looked at it, and looked at the dog, and put the sodden thing back on his own head so that river water ran down his face like tears.

*Well,* he had said. *What am I going to call you, then?*

And then, after a while, as if he had remembered something: *I know a name for a fool who runs into things.*

He had said it with the warm fall on the second half that nobody alive had ever heard, and the young dog's tail had gone before anything else in him could, and that was the day he became Romeo.

Romeo lay on the ice three thousand years later and turned the day over like a bone.

That was what was in the pockets. Not the name. The name was a coat; he had a better one underneath. What was in the pockets was the cold, and the brown, and the not-hearing, and an old man sitting in the mud putting a wet hat on his head because a dog had brought it back. If he bit the thread, the stranger would have no more use of the name, and Romeo would have no more use of that afternoon. He would remember a river. He would not remember why he had gone in.

*And it knows that,* he thought. *It has read the day. It knows exactly what's in the pockets. That's why it tied the knot with this name and not another.*

Then, more slowly, with his ears going flat against the ice: *That's why the paw moved.*

Because it had not felt like silver, the pull. It had not felt like the chain at the Gate. It had felt like a hat going away on brown water. It had felt like something of Shen's leaving, and a fool who runs into things getting up to go after it.

*Clever,* thought Romeo, and hated it very precisely.

---

Ling Mei woke in the middle of the afternoon, all at once, the way she did, with her hand still in his scruff.

She looked at the hand. She looked at him. She took the hand back and flexed it, and he heard the knuckles crack.

"How long was I holding that?"

⟨Since the middle of the morning.⟩

"Why?"

He told her. He did not make it smaller. He told her about the paw, and the second paw, and the patient pull on something behind his breastbone, and the System's word *partial*, and that her hand had been in his scruff the whole time and it had helped more than he could explain. He told her about the knot, and the name tied into it, and what was in the pockets, and that biting it would cost him the river.

She listened the way she listened to everything, all the way to the end, sitting up on Bao's warm patch with the coat round her shoulders and her crooked arm in her lap.

"So on the dark night," said Ling Mei, "it can just walk you."

⟨It can try.⟩

"It *will* try. You said it was patient." She rubbed her face with both hands. "And you can't cut it."

⟨I can. I would rather not.⟩

"That's the same thing, at your age," said Ling Mei. "You told me."

He had told her that. He was beginning to regret telling her anything.

She sat for a while looking at the shore. Then she reached into the front of her coat and took out the long hemp cord the junior steward had given her, the one that tied to the harness round his ribs, and began without a word to knot it to the ring behind his shoulders.

Romeo looked at the cord. Every hair along his red saddle rose.

⟨Ling Mei.⟩

"Mm."

⟨I am three thousand one hundred and twelve years old. I have been at the Ninth Gate. I do not go on a lead.⟩

"You're on one now." She tied the other end round her own left wrist, the crooked one, twice, and pulled the knot tight with her teeth. "If something's going to walk you, it can walk me as well. I'm heavier than you."

⟨Not by as much as you think.⟩

"I carried you over a mountain," said Ling Mei. "I know exactly how much."

He opened his mouth to say something about dignity, and found that he did not have it in him. The cord lay between them in the snow, slack, smelling of well rope and the junior steward's anxious hands. It went round his ribs, not his neck. It had nobody's name in it.

**\[Tether noted. Material: hemp.\]**  
**\[Binding: none.\]**

*I can see that,* thought Romeo. The System withdrew a little faster than usual, which he chose to take as tact.

"Good," said Ling Mei, who could not see the panel but could see his face. "Now. Tell me which way we're going home."

---

They went at dusk, as the sky over the long white shoulder turned the colour of a bruise.

She carried him for the first part, inside her coat with the cord looped round her wrist and his chin on her collar, because the snow along the shore had drifted deep in the storm and his legs would have vanished in it to the shoulder. He let her. At the edge of the ice he twisted round inside the coat to look back once at the black crack and the grey patch where she had slept, and sent down into the lake the only thing he had to send.

⟨Sleep, old brother. I'll knock louder next time.⟩

Nothing came back. He had not expected it to.

They had gone perhaps two hundred paces up the slope when the thread moved.

It did not pull. He was braced for a pull, every claw out inside her coat, and none came. Instead, under the snow, under the ice behind them and the rock ahead, he felt the long humming line of his own name begin to *slide*. It went the way a rope goes over the edge of a well when somebody at the bottom starts walking off with the end of it: smooth and steady and not in any hurry, away from the slot, into the mountain, down.

Down the dry passage. Toward the spring and the seam and the pin.

Toward the sect.

Ling Mei felt him go rigid. "What?"

⟨It's leaving.⟩

"Good."

⟨No,⟩ said Romeo. He lifted his nose into the dark and could smell nothing at all, which was the point. ⟨It isn't waiting for us any more. It's going home ahead of us.⟩

**\[Holder: moving.\]**  
**\[Direction: Azure Wolf Sect.\]**

Under her boots, under the snow, the thread went on sliding south and down through the mountain, a whole night's walk faster than they could follow, toward a sleeping yard, a lamp beside a stone, and a hut with nobody at all sitting on its third floorboard.
