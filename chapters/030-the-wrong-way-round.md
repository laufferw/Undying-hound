# **Chapter 30: The Wrong Way Round**

Ling Mei drew eleven buckets before dark, which was eleven more than anyone had expected of a girl who had spent the afternoon being questioned by an elder with a whole dog's worth of qi rolling round inside her ribs.  
The junior steward wrote them in his book. He looked at the number for a while. "Well," he said, and went away to eat his turnip.  
Romeo spent those hours in the den, flat on the trough, with nothing in him. It was a strange feeling, being empty on purpose. The earth qi came up through the frost-scarred clay the way it always did, cool and patient, and found no sea to fill, only the shape where one had been, and lay there in a thin film like rain in a footprint. His back made a long private remark about the drain, the stair and the coat. He let it.  
When the light went, he crawled the forty level paces of the back-door tunnel on his belly, and she was waiting in the ditch reeds with her sleeves still wet to the elbow.  
"You can come in," she said. "Properly. Through the door, like a guest."  
*I am not a guest,* said Romeo, as she lifted him out. *I am a tenant. I live in the basement.*  
"You're a lodger who doesn't pay," said Ling Mei, "and who put his soup in me."  
---
The hut was as cold as a well. There was no charcoal in the bowl and no lamp, and the sleeping platform stood against the wall at a height that, to Romeo, was a cliff. Ling Mei did not try to put him on it. She spread her blanket over the third floorboard, which had become, without anyone deciding it, the most important piece of wood in the Azure Wolf Sect, and lay down on her side, and set him in the curve of her, his long back against her stomach and his head under her chin.  
"Take it, then," she whispered. "Before I get used to it."  
He reached down the bond for his qi.  
It did not come.  
He had pushed it into her all at once, in the time of a breath, the way you throw a coat over a lamp. Now he went looking for it, and found that the thing he had thrown was no longer there in a heap. Ling Mei's tide had done what it did to everything: taken it down and in, round and under, the wrong way, and spread it through her meridians in threads so thin that his own qi no longer felt like his. Pulling on the bond only made her gasp, and the threads went deeper, as fish do when you slap the water.  
*Hm,* said Romeo.  
"That's not a good sound."  
*It's a thinking sound.*  
"It's the sound the physician makes before he says 'interesting.'"  
He lay still, with his nose against the pulse in her throat, and did the thing he should have thought of first, because it was the thing he had done for three thousand years.  
He smelled for it.  
Not her tide; he knew her tide, cold and slow and deep, ink and well rope and the iron of a cold hut. Under it, if he let it come to his nose, there was something else. Small, warm, a little dusty. Earth and clay and the trough and, faintly, absurdly, wet dog. His own qi, smelling of where it had been.  
*Nine Scents, first layer,* he thought. *A scent can be followed through water.*  
Her tide was water of a kind. So he followed it.  
It was the strangest road he had ever walked. He went into her the wrong way round, down when everything in him wanted to go up, in when he wanted to go out, following his own smell along her channels the way he had once followed a drowned hat down the bed of a river. The threads were everywhere: tucked behind her heart, wound round the old crookedness of her left elbow, lying along the backs of her ribs like warm string. He did not pull them. He went to each one, one at a time, and let it know him, and it came.  
It took a long time. Somewhere in the middle of it, Ling Mei's breathing changed.  
"It's going," she whispered. "Oh. That's horrible. It's like someone taking a hot stone out of your bed."  
*I'll be quick.*  
"Don't be quick. Be careful." A pause. "It's colder without it."  
*It always is,* said Romeo, and went on.  
---
When he had perhaps half of it back, she said, into the dark above his head, "What did the wolf say to you?"  
He had known she would ask. She had felt his tail go still against her ribs in the yard. A girl who could count to twenty-four buckets on no sleep could count a dog's heartbeats.  
So he told her. The window. The old word for snow. The name under Brow's coat, which Brow had never said aloud to anyone in his life, said aloud under a window at night by a thing that had none of its own.  
*In his mother's voice,* said Romeo.  
Ling Mei was quiet for so long that he thought she had fallen asleep with his qi in her. Then she said, very low:  
"It used Ah-Shu's mother, too."  
*Yes.*  
"And it used your old man. Shen. At your hole." Her breath moved the fur between his ears. "And it used you. On me."  
Romeo stopped walking her channels.  
"It doesn't just steal voices," said Ling Mei. "It steals the one you'd open the door for."  
He lay against her in the cold and turned that over, the way he had turned over a great many things in three thousand years, and found nothing wrong with it anywhere. It was exactly right. It was the sort of thing he ought to have said himself, two days ago, and had not, because he had been too busy being bitten by the past to notice the shape of the teeth.  
*You,* he said, *are going to be very annoying when you're old.*  
"I'm annoying now."  
*Yes. But you'll have practice.*  
He went back to work. The last threads were the hardest, lying deep along the bottom of her tide where the cold was oldest, and when he coaxed the final one loose it came up out of her and into him all at once, a whole small warm sea rushing back into its basin.  
It was not the same sea.  
It had gone round her the wrong way and come back the right way, and somewhere on that journey it had been turned over, the way a field is turned over, the way a river stone is turned over by ten thousand years of water. It was denser. Quieter. It sat in his channels the way a good dog sits by a fire: all the way down, without fidgeting.

Romeo read it twice. He had not risen a level since the night the knock came, and he had not risen one at all, in any life, by being carried round inside someone else.  
*Huh,* he said.  
"Is that the interesting sound?"  
*That's the sound of an old dog being surprised.* He did not tell her what had surprised him. He told her something else, which was also true: *You did that. Your tide did. It washed it.*  
"Good," said Ling Mei. "Then you owe me rent."  
---
For a while neither of them spoke. Outside, wind went down the lane and pushed snow under the door in a thin white line. From the kennels, far off, a wolf made one short sound and stopped, as if it had forgotten what it meant to say.  
"I've decided," said Ling Mei.  
*About the turtle?*  
"About the other thing. The name."  
Romeo kept very still.  
"Not one I'd tell you," she said quickly. "Not one I'd tell anybody. You said it only works if it's never said out loud. So it won't be." She shifted her crooked arm under her head. "I just thought, if that thing ever stands outside this door using somebody's voice, the one I'd open it for, and calls me, then at least it'll be calling someone who isn't all of me."  
*That's not how names are usually chosen,* said Romeo. *Usually there is a ceremony. Incense. A grandfather who can't remember why.*  
"Well, I've got a dog and a cold floor."  
*That,* said Romeo, *is how I chose mine.*  
She did not ask about that. He felt her not ask, and loved her for it, which was inconvenient, and which he had known was coming since the bun.  
Then she was quiet in a different way, the way a person is quiet when they are inside their own head, turning something in their fingers. Romeo did not try to listen. He shut his ears, which on a dachshund is a matter of letting them lie, and kept his nose politely in her collar.  
But a nose cannot be shut.  
So he smelled it, when it happened. Under the scent of Ling Mei, that he knew as well as earth by now, a second thing, very small, very new. Like a seed in a pocket. Whole. Unbitten. Not leaning anywhere at all.

Romeo let out a long breath through his nose and did not say anything, because there was nothing to say that would not have been about it, and anything said about it was a little too close to saying it.  
"There," said Ling Mei. "Done. Don't ask."  
*I wasn't going to.*  
"You were. You're very rude."  
*I'm very old.*  
"Same thing," said Ling Mei, and he heard her smiling.  
---
They made the plan the way poor people make plans, which is with what they had.  
Fang wanted the dog in his room at dusk on the dark night. Ah-Shu wanted the dog at the bottom of the dry passage on the dark night. The thing with no smell wanted the dog at the top of it. The collector wanted every beast on the ledger chained round the stone. That was four people wanting one small dog in four places on one night, and Romeo had not even asked the turtle yet.  
"Then ask him," said Ling Mei. "Tomorrow night."  
*Over the shoulder of the mountain? It's most of a night by dark, Ling Mei. And deep snow. You've seen me in deep snow. I am a small red plough.*  
"I've seen you in a coat," said Ling Mei. "You fit."  
*Your buckets—*  
"I'll draw them in the morning, and I'll carry you in the night, and I'll sleep the day after. Fang's order is for the dark night. Nobody said anything about the night before." She yawned against the top of his head. "You promised the wolf you'd ask a turtle before you did something stupid. I heard you not saying it. So you're asking the turtle."  
He could not, at the moment, find a single thing wrong with that either.  
She fell asleep before he had stopped looking.  
---
He lay awake in the curve of her, warm for once, with a sea in him that sat all the way down, and checked himself over the way an old general counts his men after a battle that has not yet happened.

Level 4, with a strained back, a split eyebrow, no food in two days but half a turnip, and three nights left. It was not a great deal. It was more than he had had that morning. He had climbed out of worse holes with less, eleven times.  
His eyes had nearly closed when the voice came down the hill.  
*Small dog.*  
Faint, like snow sliding off a far roof. Mind to mind, only to him.  
Romeo lifted his head off Ling Mei's arm. *Brow.*  
The wolf did not answer at once. When he did, his rusty-gate voice was so even that it frightened Romeo more than a howl would have.  
*It came back. Under the window. An hour ago.* A pause. *He was asleep. He did not hear it.*  
*Brow—*  
*It said my name again. In her voice. The way she said it when I was blind and could not find her in the den.*  
The wind pushed more snow under the door. Ling Mei slept on.  
*Small dog,* said Brow, very quietly, *I answered.*
