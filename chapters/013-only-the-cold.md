# **Chapter 13: Only the Cold**

Romeo did not move.  
It was the hardest thing he had done in three thousand years, and he had once sat for forty days on Mount Gaoqian without food. Every hair on his back was standing. Every nerve in his neck was screaming at him to twist, to wrench his head away from the silver, to get out from under the hand of the current before something closed around his throat. The flinch was in him like a second heartbeat. He held it the way he had once held a bone in his mouth all night because a bigger dog was watching.  
The water pinned him to the pin. The roof of the channel here came down to within a finger's width of the surface, and he had tilted his nose up into that sliver of air and stayed there, nostrils just clear, the rest of him under. He breathed through the tip of his nose, very slowly. He did not paddle. He did not shiver, though that was less discipline than the fact that his body had given up on shivering some time ago.  
The tap came again. One tap. Light, unhurried, like a fingernail on a teacup.  
The pin rang with it. Romeo felt the note go through his ribs and into his teeth and up into the thread, and he smelled the thread carry it away up the dark channel, toward the lake, toward Bao, singing a little louder for a moment before it settled.  
Then light.  
Not much. A hairline seam in the rock beside the pin, thinner than a whisker, that he had taken for a vein of quartz, began to glow a soft and steady yellow. Lamp light. On the other side of the stone was not stone but space: a dry passage running alongside the river, close enough that the light came through the crack. Close enough that he could smell the lamp. Close enough that he could smell, under it, silk.  
The stranger did not smell of anything else. It never had. No sweat, no breath, no hunger, no fear, no intent. Romeo's nose, which had smelled lies in the mouths of emperors, slid off it like a paw off ice.  
"Still singing," said the light, ageless voice, and the words arrived flat and dead against the stone, with no echo at all. "Good."  
A pause. In it Romeo heard the hiss of the lamp wick and the slow, drowning pulse of his own blood.  
"Hm," said the stranger.  
The light leaned closer to the crack. Romeo could not see it. He could only smell the lamp smoke thicken and feel, against the pin, a faint new attention, the way a spider's thread tightens when the spider puts a foot on it. Something was listening along the silver. Something was feeling for what was touching it.  
*Do not move,* Romeo told every part of himself. *Do not wag.*  
His tail, underwater and numb and very nearly dead to him, considered it. He felt it consider it. Then it decided, for once in its life, to obey.  
A blue panel unfolded in the dark in front of his eyes. He would have shut his eyes if he had dared to move his eyelids.  
**\[WARNING\]**  
**\[Condition: Hypothermia (severe). VIT and AGI greatly reduced. Body heat: negligible.\]**  
**\[Recommendation: leave the water immediately.\]**  
The panel did not linger, this time. It folded at once, as quietly as it had come, the way a servant steps out of a doorway when a guest the household fears walks past.  
"Cold," said the voice on the other side of the stone. It said the word as if tasting it. "Only the cold."  
The light withdrew from the crack. Slow footsteps, soft as slippers, moved away along the dry passage, and they did not go down the mountain. They went up. Toward the lake. The lamp glow thinned along the crack and faded and was gone, and somewhere far above, very faint, Romeo heard a second tap on a second pin, and then, much later and fainter, a third.  
*Walking the pins,* he thought. *Before the new moon. Checking its knots.*  
And it had touched the one he was lying on, and felt nothing there but a cold so deep it did not register as a living thing. Bao had said a collector looks for what is missing. Romeo had been missing the one thing every living creature carries. He had been missing warmth.  
He was going to die of the thing that had saved him, if he did not get out of this water.  
---

He could not go forward; the current had him against the outside of the bend like a coin pressed into a table. He could not go up; there was a finger of air and then rock. He would not go back. That left one direction, and it was the one direction he had never refused.  
*Under,* thought Romeo, and something in his chest, very small and very old, wagged.  
The floor of the bend, just below the pin, was not bedrock. The river had dropped its gravel there for a thousand years, the way rivers always do at the outside of a turn, and Romeo's front paws found it the moment he stopped trying to keep them still. Coarse grit. Pebbles. Loose.  
He dug.  
A dachshund digging underwater looks exactly as foolish as it sounds. He could not see. He could barely feel his paws. He dug with his head held up into the sliver of air and his body angled down like a badger-dog going into an earth, front legs going in short, furious, alternating strokes, and the current, which had been his enemy for the last hundred breaths, became his labourer. Everything he loosened, it carried away. Grit and pebbles went streaming past his ribs and off downstream. He felt the bottom drop a claw's depth. Then a paw's. He kept his neck well clear of the pin and his whole body clear of the place, higher on the metal, where the thread went in. He kept his mind clear of the chain.  
The pin, he noticed, did not go very deep. Its point sat in a cleft in the bedrock under the gravel, wedged rather than rooted. A dog with time and warmth and nothing to lose could have worked it loose in an afternoon.  
*Not today,* he told himself. *A pin that falls is a pin that gets noticed. Not today, old man. Remember where it is.*  
The gravel gave way all at once. The current, finding a lower road, poured into the hole he had made and took Romeo with it, scraping him down along the bottom of the bend, under the pin and under a lip of stone and out the other side into the next stretch of channel, spinning, battered, free.  
He let himself go long again, ears flat, legs tucked. Like a sausage down a drain.  
---

The rest of the river was kinder, or he was too cold to tell the difference. Two more air pockets. In the second, wider than the others, a ledge of stone stuck out just above the water, and Romeo hauled himself onto it with his elbows, the way he climbed onto anything, which was badly and with tremendous resolve.  
He lay there and steamed, faintly, which he took as a good sign. Then he untied the knot of the lunch bundle with his teeth.  
The last salted rice ball was no longer a rice ball. It was a sort of grey paste in the shape of a regret. Romeo ate it in two bites and licked the cloth and then licked the cloth again, because there was still salt in the weave and he was a dog and nobody was watching. Food went into him and something in his belly lit, like one coal blown on in a dead stove. He could feel his tail again. It wagged, without consulting him. He let it.  
It was while he was licking the rice off his own chest that he found the silver.  
A tiny curl of it. No longer than a grain of rice and thinner than a fingernail paring, caught in the white fur over his breastbone. The stranger's tap had flaked it from the head of the pin, and the current had pressed it into him while he lay there being dead. It smelled of silk and lamp smoke and, under those, the thin bright smell his nose had once filed away without asking him: *Silver (bound).*  
Every instinct he had told him to bite it out. To spit it into the black water and scrub his chest on the stone until the smell was gone. It made his teeth ache just being near it. It made the skin under it itch with the memory of a chain.  
*Wear a little of what you hate,* Bao had said.  
Romeo looked at the silver for a long time. Then, with infinite care, using only his front teeth, he lifted it out of his fur and laid it in the centre of the blue cloth and folded the cloth over it twice and tied the slip-knot again, tight, and put the bundle back around his neck. It sat against his throat, exactly where he could not bear anything to sit.  
He hated it. He wore it.  
**\[Item acquired: Silver shaving (bound).\]**  
**\[Source: unknown.\]**  
**\[Effect: unknown.\]**  
*Liar,* Romeo told the System. *You know exactly what it does.*  
The System, impartial, said nothing at all.  
---

The channel ended where seams end: in a crack too narrow for water to bother with, so the water stopped bothering and simply soaked away into the earth. Romeo came to it in the late afternoon, by the feel of the air pockets, and found himself in a wet slot of rock with the river vanishing around his paws into mud.  
He put his nose to the mud. Earth qi. Clean, mineral, cold, running straight down the hill along the seam, exactly as it had run up. And, very faint, from somewhere ahead and above and to the left, a smell that made him stop breathing for an entirely different reason.  
Old cedar floorboards. Bitterroot. Burnt millet. A girl who had been awake all night.  
He dug.  
It took him the rest of the day. The mud was heavy and the stones in it were stubborn and his back, his long weak back, began to complain somewhere in the second hour and did not stop. Twice he had to lie down in his own tunnel and simply breathe. Once a root as thick as his leg blocked the way and he chewed through it, which took a very long time and tasted of nothing. He did not hurry. He did not stop. He went forward the way Old Man Shen had once gone up the mountain after herbs on bad knees: one step, and then the next one, and no thought at all for the top.  
Some time after dark, his paws broke into a space he knew.  
A round chamber. A smooth trough down the middle of it, where the earth-qi vein ran clean and cold. A shaft going up, rubbed raw with bitterroot. At the bottom of the shaft, folded the way a dog folds a thing to sleep on, a girl's oldest under-robe.  
He had come home through the floor of his own den.  
**\[Quest: Anything Less Than the End of the World\]**  
**\[Remaining condition: return before the naming.\]**  
**\[Time remaining: 13 hours.\]**  
Romeo crawled onto the robe. It smelled of her. He was too tired to bark, so he did the next best thing. He put his chest flat against the stone of the shaft, the way he had on the mountain, and gave one small, cracked, badger-dog *wuff*.  
The floorboard came up so fast it hit the wall.  
Ling Mei's face appeared at the top of the shaft, upside down in candlelight, and for a moment she did not say anything at all. Then she reached down the shaft with both arms, the bad left hand and the shaking right one, and Romeo, who could not jump onto the sleeping platform and could not jump out of a shaft either, stood up on his short back legs and let her take him.  
"You horrible old sock," she said into his wet ear. "You horrible, horrible old sock."  
She had the warming powder in a bowl of water before he had finished shaking himself across her floor, and she held it while he drank, and the warmth went down into him like a hand closing around a candle. He drank it all. Then he licked the bowl. Then he licked her fingers, the right hand, the shaking one, which was shaking less.  
She wrapped him in her blanket and sat with him on her lap, and her tide, which had leaned uphill all day, came back down and settled around him like water finding its level. Romeo felt it with his whole body and did not say anything. Some things are said better by lying very still on someone's knees.  
Then her nose wrinkled.  
"You smell different," she said slowly. "You smell like..." She frowned. "Like the kennels. Like the wolves."  
*Good,* said Romeo, into her mind, and felt her start. *That's the idea. I'll explain. I need to sleep for about a year first.*  
He did not get a year. He did not get an hour.  
Someone knocked on the door. Not a kick, so not Zhao Peng. Three knocks, measured and patient, the knocks of a man who has never in his life had to knock twice.  
Romeo's nose told him who it was before Ling Mei's face did. Wolf, great and blue-white and leaning. Pine resin and old ink. Nascent Soul qi, folded tight and very calm.  
"Disciple Ling," said Elder Fang Wuji through the door. "I find I can't wait for the morning. I'd like to see the dog."
