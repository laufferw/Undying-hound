# **Chapter 32: Weight in the Wrong Places**

Romeo did not turn round.

It was the hardest thing he had done all week, and the week had included a blizzard. Every hair along his red saddle stood up on its own. His ears, which were long enough to be a nuisance at the best of times, had swung forward against the ice like two hands trying to cover his eyes. Behind him, a long way back across the white, a girl was standing alone on the shore of a frozen lake, and something was standing behind her, and the best nose in the Ninefold Mountains could not find it.

⟨I smell nothing,⟩ he said.

⟨I know,⟩ said Bao. ⟨That's why I asked you. I wanted to be wrong.⟩

⟨Then how do you see it?⟩

The pause was long even for Bao. Under Romeo's chest the lake breathed once.

⟨The ice is mine,⟩ said Bao. ⟨Everything that stands on it stands on me. Your girl is a little weight, very tired, on the gravel at the edge, both feet flat. You are a long weight, low, like a log somebody left out. And behind her there is weight in the wrong places.⟩

⟨Wrong how?⟩

⟨Two heels and no toes,⟩ said Bao. ⟨Pressed in on the outside edges, deep. Like a short man with a bad knee. But it goes up much too high for a short man. And it is very close to her, little brother. It is leaning down.⟩

Romeo closed his eyes.

*Don't look for what's there,* Old Man Shen had said once, in a kitchen with a stolen pot in it. *Look for the pot.*

So he stopped trying to smell the shore and smelled the wind instead. It came down the long fall of the mountain behind them, over the stones, over Ling Mei, out across the ice, carrying everything it touched: snow, iron, wet wool, ink, well rope, the warm salt of a girl who has walked all night. He let it come to his nose. He followed each thread of it back to where it had started.

And there, one pace behind Ling Mei, the wind had a hole in it.

Not a shape. The open was too big for shapes; only in the close dark of the den had his nose ever drawn it whole. Here it was a lean. A place where the snow-smoke drifting off the slope went round something instead of through it, the way water goes round a stone, and closed up again on the far side as if nothing had been there at all.

*Borrowed,* thought Romeo. *Not none. Not any more.* It had learned to wear Shen's walk. Now it was learning to wear his weight.

⟨It followed us,⟩ he said.

⟨Or it was waiting,⟩ said Bao. ⟨I felt something come up the passage and into the slot in the night, while you were in your hole on the mountain. It's been standing on my shore since before light.⟩ A slow breath. ⟨Waiting for you to knock, I think.⟩

---

Then it spoke.

It did not speak to Romeo. It spoke aloud, close behind Ling Mei's ear, in a voice that carried beautifully across the ice in the still dawn: a dry old voice, a little pompous, the voice of a man reading out his own will and enjoying the vocabulary.

"Ling Mei," said Romeo's voice. "Don't move. The ice is rotten out here. Stay exactly where you are, and I'll come back to you."

Romeo's lip went up off his teeth. It was a very good imitation, better than the one in the den. It had his pauses now, and the little lift on *exactly* that he had not known he did.

He put his own voice into her mind, low and plain, with no lift in it anywhere.

⟨Ling Mei. That isn't me. Don't answer it. Don't turn round. Walk out to me, please. Slowly, in a straight line.⟩

He could not see her, and he would not turn. He lay on the ice with his eyes shut and his nose on the wind and listened to her with his whole body, the way he had listened to her through the third floorboard on the worst night he had spent in this skin.

She did not move.

*She's tired,* he thought. *She has been awake a day and a night and half of another. She's frozen. And she has two voices, both mine, telling her opposite things.* A spoken voice was not him; she knew that; she had made it her test. But a test is a thing you make in a hut with a lamp lit, and this was a frozen lake at dawn with something that did not breathe breathing on the back of her neck.

"Ling Mei," said the voice behind her, patiently. "It's me."

And Ling Mei sniffed.

It carried across the ice as clear as a bell: a short, wet, scornful sniff, the sniff of a girl from the outer courtyard who has been asked to believe something stupid by someone who ought to know better.

Then she walked out onto the ice.

---

She came in a straight line, as he had asked, her crooked left arm held a little out from her side for balance. The gravel crunched. Then the ice took her and creaked and did not mind her.

Behind her, the ice creaked again.

⟨It's following,⟩ said Bao.

Romeo heard it now, through his chest. Her steps, light and uneven, the right foot coming down harder because her left side was weak; and after each of hers, a second step that was not a step. Heel. Drag. Heel. Pressed in on the outer edges. A short man with a bad knee who was much too tall, walking in the footprints of a girl, the way a small dog had once walked in his.

*It wanted her to stay on the shore,* thought Romeo. *Rotten ice, it said. And now she's coming, and it's coming after her, onto—*

⟨Onto me,⟩ said Bao, and for the first time in three thousand years Romeo heard the old turtle angry.

It was not a loud anger. Nothing about Bao was loud. It came up through the lake like the first slow turn of a season, and the whole sheet of ice under Romeo's chest drew taut as a drumskin.

⟨You don't walk on my lake,⟩ said Bao, to something that was not Romeo. ⟨Not in his feet. Not in anybody's.⟩

⟨Bao. Your toe—⟩

⟨Is mine,⟩ said Bao. ⟨Let's see what it's good for.⟩

The ice boomed.

Romeo felt it in his teeth and his long back and the roots of his ears: one deep note, like a temple bell struck from underneath. A crack shot out from beneath him toward the shore, straight and black and fast. It passed under Ling Mei's feet, and she staggered and did not fall, and behind her heels it opened, a hand's width, then a forearm's. Lake water came welling up through it, black and smoking with cold, and with the water came Bao's qi, deep and slow and ten thousand summers old, pouring up into the dawn like steam off a horse.

The wrong weight stopped at the edge of the water.

Romeo felt it stop. Two heels pressed into the ice on the lip of the crack, deep on their outer edges, and stayed there. And the water, which goes everywhere and touches everything, rose against the place where the rest of it should have been, and found it.

For one heartbeat, through Bao, Romeo knew its outline. Tall. Narrow. Bent at the top like a heron over a pond. And something at the end of one long arm, held out over Ling Mei's shoulder, that had been about to touch her hair.

Then it spoke once more, and not in Romeo's voice.

"Bao," it said. It was a woman's voice, low and amused and a little hoarse, the voice of somebody who had sat on a great many riverbanks and laughed at a great many things. "Look at you. You've got so *fat*."

Under the ice, Bao went utterly still.

The crack stopped widening. The welling water sank back. The lake that had been taut as a drum went slack all at once, like a held breath let go, and the silence that came up through Romeo's ribs was not one of Bao's slow silences. It was the other kind. The closed-door kind.

*The one other,* thought Romeo. *Gone a very long time.*

The heels on the lip of the crack drained away into the ice the way water drains out of a footprint. The hole in the wind closed. The snow-smoke off the slope came straight through where it had been, and behind Ling Mei there was nothing but a seam of black water and the white flank of the mountain going up into the morning.

---

She reached him on her hands and knees, the last few body-lengths, because her legs had decided they were finished. She lay down flat beside him, spread out the way he had once told her to spread her weight on ice, and put her face down on her sleeve.

"That," she said, muffled, "was you being pompous again."

⟨It was not me.⟩

"I know. It said *exactly* the way you do." She turned her head so that one dark eye looked at him across the ice. "The real one said please."

Romeo had nothing to say to that. He crawled the last half body-length and laid his long side along hers, chin on her shoulder, and sent a single thread of qi down the bond and walked it round her the wrong way and home again. She shut her eyes.

⟨Bao,⟩ he said.

Nothing.

⟨Bao. Who was that?⟩

The silence went on so long that Romeo began, in spite of himself, to be afraid of it.

⟨Nobody,⟩ said Bao at last. His voice was a long way down and very tired. ⟨Somebody. A coat it found. Leave it, little brother.⟩ A breath. ⟨It worked, anyway. I stopped. That's what it was for.⟩

⟨Stopped what?⟩

⟨Holding,⟩ said Bao. ⟨I had my toe out of the mud and all of my mind in the water, everything I've got pressed up against the bottom of the ice. And when it said that, I forgot to hold.⟩

Romeo lifted his head.

⟨Go and smell the crack,⟩ said Bao.

He went on his belly, ears trailing, his back muttering at every push, to where the black seam ran through the ice an arm's length from his nose. The water in it smoked. He put his muzzle down close, where the cold came up off it like a blade, and breathed.

He knew the threads. He had lain under this lake in the warm rich dark with one of them humming above his head, and bitten it, and felt two more snap far off as a turtle shrugged; twenty-seven once, and then twenty-four, and one cold toe on a back left foot that Bao had forgotten could be cold. He knew how they smelled. Silver, and something sewn into the silver.

There was a new one.

It came up out of the crack beside his nose, thin and very tight and very fresh, singing like a plucked hair. It ran down through the black water to somewhere near the turtle's back left foot, where a free toe had been a moment ago, and the other way it ran along the underside of the ice toward the shore, toward the slot, toward the passage and the mountain and the night after next.

It was tied, like the others, with a name.

Romeo breathed it in before he could stop himself. Cold water moving over stones in the sun. Mud and reeds. Warm wet fur, three thousand years younger. An old man's sleeve. And, faint and sweet and unmistakable, straw, and red cotton thread.
