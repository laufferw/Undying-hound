# **Chapter 35: Marked Present**

The storm had used up all its snow two nights before, and what was left behind it was the cold.

It was a clear cold, the kind that comes down out of a sky with nothing in it but stars. There were so many of them over the long white shoulder that the snow had a light of its own, grey-blue and faint, and Ling Mei's shadow went up the slope ahead of her like a second, thinner girl. The wind had dropped to nothing. Every sound they made went out across the mountain and did not come back.

Romeo lasted inside her coat until the first rise.

⟨Put me down.⟩

"No."

⟨The snow has a crust on it now. Feel it.⟩

Ling Mei stopped and pressed one boot down. The surface held for a heartbeat, creaked, and let her through to the knee with a sound like a biscuit breaking. She looked at the hole, and then down the front of her coat at him.

"It does not have a crust on it."

⟨It does for me.⟩

She thought about that. Then she unbuttoned the coat, lifted him out by the harness like a parcel, and set him on the snow beside her leg. He spread his paws. The crust took him. He stood on top of the mountain on four short legs with his ears out and his long belly a finger's width clear of the surface, and he felt, for the first time since the lake, slightly superior.

"Don't," said Ling Mei.

⟨I have not said anything.⟩

"You were going to say something about being the correct length."

⟨I am the correct length,⟩ said Romeo. ⟨Tonight the mountain agrees with me.⟩

So they went on like that: Romeo trotting across the top of the snow with the hemp cord paying out behind him, Ling Mei breaking through it at every step and pulling her legs out and breaking through again, her breath going up in clouds. Where the crust was thin he went slowly, testing it with a forepaw the way he tested ice, and she learned to put her feet in the places he had tested. Where it was thick he went ahead to the end of the cord and waited, sitting very upright, looking back at her with his head tilted.

"If you look at me like that again," said Ling Mei, wading, "I will carry you upside down."

⟨I am encouraging you.⟩

"You look like a dog waiting for dinner."

⟨That is what encouragement looks like on this face.⟩

He did not tell her that every time he stopped, he was also listening.

Under the snow and the rock, the thread tied with his name ran away from them down the inside of the mountain. He could feel it the way you feel a draught on the back of your neck. It no longer slid. Somewhere in the second hour it had stopped moving altogether and gone still and taut, pointing south and down, and he knew without being told that whatever held the other end of it had reached the bottom of the dry passage. Had passed the spring. Had come up through the seam, or out of the crack beside the pin, and was standing now in the sect, holding his name like a lantern, waiting for him to come home.

*Let it wait,* he thought. *I have done a great deal of waiting in my life. I am better at it than anybody.*

His stomach, which had eaten half a bun in two days, made a private remark about that.

---

Around midnight Ling Mei brought up the map.

He had known it was coming. She had saved it like a good coin since the night he told her about the drain, and she spent it now, on a steep stretch where she had to stop every twenty paces to breathe, which meant she had plenty of time between sentences.

"You said yes to a trap," said Ling Mei.

⟨I said yes to a map.⟩

"You said that was a map because you knew where the trap was." She pulled her leg out of the snow. "Now it's got a lead on you. So the trap knows where *you* are. That isn't a map. That's a trap with a lead on it."

Romeo considered several replies, most of them three thousand years old and all of them pompous.

⟨Two leads,⟩ he said at last. ⟨Ah-Shu's hand and my name. Both of them want me in the same place.⟩

"And you still want to go."

⟨I still want to go.⟩

She was quiet for twenty paces. Then she lifted her left wrist, the crooked one, with the cord wound round it twice, and let it fall again so that the line between them jumped in the starlight.

"Then I'm the third," said Ling Mei. "And I don't care where anybody wants you. Tell me that's on your map."

⟨It is now,⟩ said Romeo, and found to his annoyance that his tail had agreed with her before he had.

---

The crest of the shoulder was bare rock and old ice, scoured by the storm. Below it on the near side the slope fell away steep and smooth for a long way before it broke over the lip of a gully, and the crust there was not snow any more. It was glass.

He felt the difference through his pads before she did. ⟨Ling Mei. Slowly. Go left, toward the rocks.⟩

She went left. She was slow. It did not matter.

Her boot came down on a place where the wind had polished the crust smooth as a bowl, and it did not break, and it did not hold either. Her foot went out from under her. She sat down hard, and then she was not sitting, she was sliding, on her back on the glass, fast, the coat hissing on the ice, her good hand clawing at nothing, going down toward the gully lip and the dark beyond it.

The cord whipped out straight.

Romeo had perhaps half a heartbeat. In half a heartbeat a sensible animal his size, tied by the ribs to a falling girl, would have been plucked off the mountain like a burr off a sleeve. Romeo was not a sensible animal. He was a dachshund. There was a spur of black rock beside him, the size of a stove, with its root deep in the mountain, and every instinct he had ever been bred with looked at that rock and saw a hole to go to ground behind.

He went round it.

Not over; he could not have gone over it. Round it, low and fast and the wrong way, so that the cord came across the face of the rock behind him with a sound like a saw, and caught, and wrapped. Then he went flat on the far side with his chest and his belly and his chin on the ice and all four short legs out like the legs of a dropped stool, and drove his claws in, and pushed his qi down into them.

At Lv 4 there would not have been enough. At Lv 6 there was. It went down through his shoulders and his forelegs into the five black claws of each foot like water going into roots, and the claws bit into the glass and held, and the cord came tight round the rock with all of Ling Mei's sliding weight on the end of it.

It nearly tore him in half.

The rock took most of it. Rope round a rock is a very old trick; Shen had used it to let baskets down cliffs. But the part the rock did not take went straight down the cord into the ring behind his shoulders and along the whole of his long back, and his back, which had been making remarks for days, stopped making remarks and screamed.

He did not let go. There was nothing to let go with. He was not holding the cord; the cord was holding him, and he was holding the mountain, and he simply declined, with every claw and every hair, to be moved.

The sliding stopped.

**\[Instinct: Go to Ground. Applied: anchorage.\]**  
**\[Load borne by tether. Load borne by host.\]**  
**\[Warning: spinal strain, aggravated.\]**

*Yes,* thought Romeo, through his teeth. *I noticed.*

Down the slope, a long way down, the cord went quiet and taut over the glass to a dark shape lying very still a body's length above the gully lip.

⟨Ling Mei.⟩

Nothing.

⟨*Ling Mei.*⟩

"I'm here." Her voice came up small and shaking. "I'm here. Don't move."

⟨I was not planning to.⟩

"My wrist." A breath. "It's all right. It's the bad one anyway."

She came up the cord hand over hand, with her good hand doing all the work and her crooked arm held against her chest, kicking toe-holes in the glass with the edges of her boots. It took a long time. Romeo lay flat behind his rock the whole while and did not move anything except his eyes, and when at last she reached the rock and dragged herself round it and lay down on the ice beside him, he found that he still could not move. His legs had locked themselves out sideways and would not come in.

Ling Mei lay on her back looking at the stars and breathing.

"You went round a rock," she said.

⟨I went to ground. The rock happened to be there.⟩

"You went round a rock with me on the end of you."

⟨It is how badgers are dealt with,⟩ said Romeo. ⟨You go in behind something and you do not come out.⟩

She turned her head and looked at him: flat, splayed, ears out, chin on the ice, a small red-and-white dog fixed to the top of a mountain like a nail.

"Can you get up?"

⟨Not at present.⟩

"Right," said Ling Mei.

She unwound the cord from round the rock and from round her own swollen wrist, very carefully, and tied it again round her waist instead. Then she gathered him up in both arms, the bad one too, slowly, with his spine kept straight along her forearm the way the physician had shown her, and put him inside her coat against her chest, where it was warm.

"I'm carrying you," she said. "Don't argue."

He did not argue. It was the first time in three thousand years he could remember not arguing, and he decided not to mention it to anyone.

---

They came over the crest at the grey end of the night.

The far side of the shoulder ran down long and white toward the first tier, and at the bottom of it, a very long way off, the Azure Wolf Sect lay small and dark in its walls, with a single yellow point burning in the middle of the outer yard. The lamp by the stone. Around it, even from here, Romeo could make out thin straight lines in the snow that had not been there three days ago. Posts. Rows of them, set in a ring.

Ling Mei stopped to look at them. She did not say anything. He felt her heart go once, hard, under his chin.

The thread lay still and taut beneath them, running down into that dark, toward the lamp, toward the ring of posts, toward the huts.

**\[Quest: Nine Days to Stop Being a Puppy.\]**  
**\[Time remaining: one night.\]**

*I know,* thought Romeo. *I can count.*

"We'll be down by the middle of the afternoon," said Ling Mei. "If I don't fall off anything else."

⟨Before dusk.⟩

"Before dusk." She started down. "Then Fang's room, and the physician saying you're ill, and you'll look ill, because you are. And then we'll see."

He put his chin back on her collar and shut his eyes, and slept, for the first time in two nights, all at once and completely, the way old dogs do when they have finally been put somewhere warm.

---

The junior steward came across the outer yard at first light with the bucket book under his arm and a pot of ink in his sleeve to keep it from freezing.

It had been a bad night in the sect. The posts had gone in after dark by lamplight, twenty-six of them, round the stone, and the wolves had howled in the kennels for an hour and then stopped all at once as though somebody had closed a door on them. He had not slept. He did not like the posts. He did not like the masked man by the lamp, who had been standing so still all night that the frost had grown on his shoulders.

He went down the lane to the hut at the end, by the woodpile, where the girl with the crooked arm lived with her sausage. He had been told she was ill, or her dog was. He had also been told to mark everyone, ill or not. He knocked.

"Outer disciple Ling Mei. Buckets."

For a moment, nothing. Then, from inside, close to the door, hoarse with sleep:

"Coming."

He knew the voice. Everyone in the outer yard knew that flat, practical voice; it had told him "twenty-four" without a tremor. He opened the book against the doorpost, wet the brush, and wrote *Ling Mei: present.*

"You're down for twelve today," he said to the door. "The Elder says go easy. Because of the dog."

"Thank you," said the voice.

He was turning away when it spoke again. Not to him. It had gone down low, as if the speaker had knelt on the floor inside and put her mouth to the boards, and it was coaxing and warm and very patient.

"Romeo," it said, into the floor. "Romeo, come up. It's cold down there. It's only me."

The junior steward smiled a little, because it was nice, he thought, the way she talked to that dog. Then he blew on the ink to dry it, and went on down the lane to the next door, and did not look back at the snow in front of the hut, where nobody had walked in or out all night.
