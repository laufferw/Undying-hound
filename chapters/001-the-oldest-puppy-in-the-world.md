# **Chapter 1: The Oldest Puppy in the World**

The first thing Romeo smelled was rain.

The second thing was himself: wet dog, singed fur, and underneath it the bitter ozone stink of lightning that had very recently tried to kill him.

The third thing was a sausage, roughly two li to the east, sizzling in pork fat with too much ginger. He filed that away for later. Three thousand years of cultivation had taught him a great many things, and the first and most important of them was: always know where the sausage is.

He opened his eyes.

Mud. A great deal of mud, and a grey sky so ordinary it was almost insulting. There was no trace of the purple tribulation clouds that had boiled over the summit of Mount Kunxu, no trace of the Ninth Heaven's Gate hanging open in the sky like a door left ajar for him. Just drizzle, a stand of bamboo, and somewhere nearby a frog with no idea who it was sharing a ditch with.

Romeo tried to stand. His legs folded under him like wet paper.

He tried again. This time he managed it, swaying, and looked down at himself. A long, low body. Stubby legs. Big white paws under a red-and-white patchwork coat that he had not seen from this angle in three millennia, because three millennia ago he had been a scrawny, half-starved little dachshund stray, and then he had become something else entirely, and now, apparently, he was a scrawny, half-starved stray again.

A pale blue panel unfolded in the air in front of his nose, the way it had every single morning for three thousand one hundred and twelve years.

**\[SYSTEM NOTICE\]**  
**\[Heavenly Tribulation: FAILED.\]**  
**\[Penalty applied: Cultivation reset. Realm reduced to Qi Gathering, Level 1.\]**  
**\[Unique Trait \[Loyal Unto Heaven's End\] has activated. Host has not died.\]**  
**\[Condolences.\]**

Romeo stared at the last line for a long time.

In all the years he had known it, the System had never once offered condolences. Not when he lost his first master. Not when the Beast Domains burned. It announced; it did not console. He decided he did not like this new development, and he especially did not like what it implied, which was that whatever had happened up there was bad enough to make heaven itself feel awkward.

He pulled up his status. It came reluctantly, as if embarrassed.

**\[STATUS\]**  
**Name: Romeo \| Race: Awakened Canine (Dachshund)**  
**Realm: Qi Gathering, Lv 1 \| Age: 3,112**  
**STR 4 · AGI 7 · VIT 6 · SPI 3 · SNS 19 · LCK 2**  
**Unique Trait: \[Loyal Unto Heaven's End\]**  
**Skills: \[Nine Scents Heavenly Tracking Sutra — Layer 1\] \[Heavenly Nose — Fragmented\] \[Buried Bone Storage — Sealed\] \[Moon-Swallowing Howl — Sealed\] \[Thousand Leash Breaker — ██████\]**

Strength four. A healthy village rooster had strength four.

He had once bitten a mountain in half. Not metaphorically. There was a valley in the fourth tier that cartographers still called Romeo's Overbite, though none of them knew why.

Luck two. Well. That one had always been bad.

He lay back down in the mud, because there was no one there to see him do it, and let the memory come.

He had been so close. The eight lesser tribulations had broken over him one after another, and he had eaten every one of them, lightning crackling between his teeth. The Ninth Gate had opened. He had smelled the air beyond it, and it had smelled like nothing he could describe, like the first morning of the world, like a field nobody had ever walked in. He had gathered himself to leap.

And then something silver had come down out of the clouds.

Not lightning. A chain. Thin, bright, patient, its links whispering against each other like coins. It had looped around his neck as gently as a collar, and a voice, calm and perfectly unbothered, had said a single word.

*"Sit."*

And his body, his three-thousand-year-old, heaven-defying body, had obeyed.

The ninth bolt had found him sitting.

Romeo's lip curled back from his small new teeth. He growled. It came out as a squeak.

He let it go. Anger was a fire, and he had nothing to feed it with at the moment. He had nothing to feed *himself* with at the moment, which brought him neatly back to the most pressing issue.

**\[Status: Starving. VIT −1 per hour until resolved.\]**

Two li east. Pork fat. Too much ginger.

Romeo got up and followed his nose.

---

The sausage, it turned out, belonged to the outer kitchen of a sect.

He knew it was a sect long before he saw it. Sects smelled of incense and ambition, of rice cooked in enormous quantities, of nervous teenagers sweating through their robes before examinations. This one smelled of something else as well: wolf. Not wild wolf, but the musk of bound spirit beasts, contract-chained and kennelled. It made the fur rise along his spine.

*Azure Wolf Sect*, he read from a weathered gate tablet as he slunk past it. *Founded by the Patriarch Fang Leiyun.*

He remembered Fang Leiyun. A promising young man, eight hundred years ago, with excellent cheekbones and a tendency to cry when he lost at Go. Romeo had once stolen his lunch. It seemed the boy had made something of himself.

The kitchen's back courtyard opened onto a refuse heap where cabbage leaves and fish bones were left to rot. Romeo had his nose deep in a promising pile of millet husks when he heard laughter. Not the good kind.

There were three of them, outer disciples in grey robes with the azure sash of their rank, and they had a fourth disciple backed against the courtyard wall. She was a girl of perhaps nineteen, thin, her robe mended at both elbows with thread that did not match. She held a steamed bun in both hands the way people hold things they cannot afford to lose.

"Rank Zero Mei," said the tallest of the three. He had a weak chin and a strong sense of his own importance, and he smelled of expensive hair oil and something else: a sharp, medicinal sweetness that Romeo's nose caught and turned over with interest. "Still here? I thought the sect cleared out its rubbish at the end of the month."

"It's the twenty-eighth, Zhao Peng," the girl said. "You've got two days to work up to it."

One of the other boys snorted, then caught Zhao Peng's look and turned it into a cough.

Zhao Peng knocked the bun out of her hands. It rolled through the mud and stopped at Romeo's paws.

Romeo looked at the bun. He looked at the girl. He looked at the boy.

Strength four, he reminded himself. He could not win a fight against three Qi Gathering disciples. He could not win a fight against the frog in the ditch, possibly. A sensible creature would eat the bun and leave.

But a nose, unlike a fist, does not need strength. It only needs to be pointed in the right direction.

He breathed in, slow and deep, the way Old Man Shen had taught him when he was a puppy, before he had ever heard of qi. The fragmented Heavenly Nose stirred like a limb waking from sleep. The world opened into layers. The rain. The rotting cabbage. The girl's fear, which was small and tired and old, and her anger, which was larger. And the medicinal sweetness on Zhao Peng's belt, which resolved itself into the unmistakable scent of a Crimson Marrow Pill, the kind brewed only in the inner sect's alchemy hall, the kind that had a scent signature unique to the elder who refined it, the kind an outer disciple could only possess if he had stolen it.

Romeo also smelled, approaching along the covered walkway behind the kitchen, a middle-aged man in the black robes of a discipline deacon, who smelled strongly of tea and of a long afternoon spent being disappointed in young people.

Romeo did the arithmetic. Then he did what dogs have always done best.

He barked.

It was not a mighty bark. It was a high, outraged, deeply undignified yap, and he aimed it straight at Zhao Peng's belt and did not stop. He bounced. He circled. He launched himself straight up, all four stubby legs leaving the ground at once, and seized the pouch's drawstring in his teeth and hauled backward with every one of his four points of strength, and the cheap knot gave way, and a small red pill in a wax seal tumbled out into the mud between Zhao Peng's boots just as the discipline deacon rounded the corner to see what all the noise was about.

There was a silence. The kind of silence that has a shape.

"Disciple Zhao," the deacon said pleasantly. "Is that a Crimson Marrow Pill bearing Elder Qiu's seal?"

Zhao Peng's mouth opened and closed. His two friends discovered urgent business elsewhere.

"Come with me," said the deacon, and Zhao Peng went, and the last thing he did before the corner swallowed him was look back at Romeo with an expression of pure, promising hatred. Romeo wagged his tail at him. He had been hated by emperors. A weak-chinned boy with hair oil was going to have to try much harder.

The courtyard emptied. The rain kept falling.

The girl slid down the wall until she was sitting on her heels, and looked at Romeo for a long moment. Then she picked the bun up out of the mud, brushed off what she could, broke it carefully in half, and held out the larger piece.

"You look worse than me," she said. "Go on."

Romeo did not move.

He knew this feeling. He had felt it three thousand years ago in a mountain village, when a poor herb gatherer with knobbly hands had crouched down and offered half a rice ball to a starving stray, and the stray had followed him home, and stayed for forty-one years, and then sat by his grave for nine more. He had felt it a few dozen times since, with a few dozen people, and he had buried every one of them.

The blue panel unfolded, gently this time.

**\[An individual has shown you kindness without expectation of reward.\]**  
**\[Your Unique Trait \[Loyal Unto Heaven's End\] resonates.\]**  
**\[Bind Companion: Ling Mei? Y / N\]**

*No*, Romeo thought firmly. *Absolutely not. Never again. I am three thousand years old and I am not doing this again.*

His tail was wagging.

He had not told it to. It was wagging all on its own, thumping wetly against the mud, the way it had always done, the way it always would, and the System, which understood dogs rather better than it let on, took it as an answer.

**\[Companion bound: Ling Mei.\]**  
**\[Spiritual voice channel unlocked.\]**

Romeo sighed through his nose. Then he stepped forward, took the bun with great delicacy, and spoke for the first time in his new life, mind to mind, in a voice that was very old and very dry.

*Thank you for the bun. My name is Romeo. I am three thousand one hundred and twelve years old, your meridians are not crippled, they are reversed, and I am going to fix them.*

Ling Mei dropped the other half of the bun.

Romeo ate that one too. He was starving, and there was no sense letting it go to waste.

---

High above the Azure Wolf Sect, far above the drizzle and the grey ordinary clouds, something thin and silver moved in the sky. A single link of chain, catching light that wasn't there, turning slowly toward the outer kitchen as though it had caught a scent.

Down in the courtyard, a blue panel opened in front of Romeo's nose, and for the first time in a very long while, the text was red.

**\[WARNING: You have been noticed.\]**  
**\[A Hound-Catcher of the Heavenly Leash has acquired your scent.\]**  
**\[Estimated time until arrival: 9 days.\]**

Romeo licked the last crumbs from his muzzle, looked up at the sky, and thought, with the calm of a creature who had outlived a hundred enemies: *Then I have nine days to stop being a puppy.*

Beside him, Ling Mei was still staring.

"Did the dog just talk?" she said, to no one in particular.

Somewhere two li away, the sausage was getting cold.
