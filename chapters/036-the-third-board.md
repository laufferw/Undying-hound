# **Chapter 36: The Third Board**

He woke because his back told him to.

It did not say anything clever about it. It simply went from a long dull ache to a bright one, all at once, the way a coal goes bright when somebody blows on it, and Romeo opened his eyes inside the front of Ling Mei's coat to grey wool and the knock of her heart and a smell of pine.

Black pines. Low ones, half buried, with snow sliding off their branches in soft lumps as she brushed past. They were off the shoulder, then. They were on the last long slope above the sect, and the light coming through the weave of her collar was the flat, tired light of an afternoon that has already decided to end early.

⟨How long,⟩ said Romeo.

"Since the crest." Ling Mei did not slow down. "You snored."

⟨I do not snore.⟩

"You whistled, then. Through your nose. Like a kettle that's ashamed of itself." She hitched him higher against her chest with her good arm, carefully, keeping his spine straight along her forearm. "How's the back?"

He thought about lying to her. It would have been easy; she could not smell lies, and he had told a great many people in three thousand years that he was perfectly well while bits of him were on fire. But she had carried him down a mountain.

⟨Bad,⟩ he said. ⟨I can feel every bone in it, and some of them are not where I left them.⟩

"Right," said Ling Mei, which was what she said instead of being frightened.

Under the snow, under the slope, under everything, the thread tied with his name ran away ahead of them, still and taut, down into the sect. It had not moved since the night. Whatever held it was waiting. He could feel it the way you feel somebody watching the back of your neck in a quiet room, and it was very close now.

---

They came in the back way, between the last two storehouses, where the snow had drifted against the walls in long blue scoops and nobody ever looked. The sect smelled the way it always did: wet wolf, cold ash, rice-water, the latrines, and a thousand outer disciples who had not bathed since the first snow. Under that was something new. Silver. A great deal of it, lying out in the cold.

The junior steward was at the well.

He saw Ling Mei come round the corner of the storehouse and his face did something complicated. Relief, and then puzzlement, and then the anxious politeness of a young man who suspects he is about to be blamed for something.

"Oh," he said. "You're up. Are you feeling better?"

Ling Mei stopped.

"Better," she said.

"Only you said this morning you'd do the buckets after you'd rested, and then at noon I knocked again and you said the same, so I didn't like to..." He looked at the bucket book as if it might help him. It said *Ling Mei: present* in his own careful brush, and below it, nothing at all. "I've put you down for none. For today. Because of the dog. The Elder said."

"Thank you," said Ling Mei.

The steward's eyes went to the lump in the front of her coat. "Is the sausage very ill?"

⟨Tell him yes,⟩ said Romeo.

"Very," said Ling Mei.

"Oh." He looked honestly sorry. "You were talking to him so nicely through the door. I thought he must be better." Then, because he was a decent young man and a nervous one, he tucked the book under his arm and fled before anybody could ask him anything else.

Ling Mei stood by the well for a long moment with the cold coming up through her boots.

"I wasn't here," she said quietly. "This morning. At noon. I was on a mountain."

⟨I know.⟩

"He said I talked to you nicely." Her voice was perfectly flat. "I don't talk to you nicely. I've never once talked to you nicely."

⟨That is true,⟩ said Romeo. ⟨It is one of your best qualities.⟩

---

She went down the lane anyway. He had known she would. It was her hut.

She did not go to the door. She went the long way round, behind the woodpile, the way Ah-Shu had stood and watched on the night of the knock, and crouched there in the snow with the logs between her and the little dark hut at the end of the row, and opened the front of her coat so that Romeo's nose was in the air.

He put his whole self into it.

Woodsmoke, old and cold; the charcoal bowl had not been lit in two days. Her blanket. Rice-water. The faint mineral breath of the den coming up through the gaps in the floor, and under it the trough, and under that the turned earth of his own tunnels. Every smell in that hut was hers or his. He had made it that way on purpose, a week ago. It was the only place in the Azure Wolf Sect that smelled like home.

And in the middle of it, where the third floorboard was, there was nothing.

Not empty. Empty has a smell; empty smells of dust and stillness and whatever was there last. This was a place where the smells went *round*. The woodsmoke bent away from it. The rice-water bent away from it. The breath of the den came up through the floor all along the room and simply did not come up there, as if something were sitting on that one board with its full borrowed weight and its knees drawn up, exactly where she had sat through so many nights, with its face turned down toward the dark.

**\[Absence detected. Position: bound companion's dwelling.\]**  
**\[Posture: seated. Attention: downward.\]**

*Yes,* thought Romeo. *I can see that. Thank you.*

Then, through the thin plank wall, softly, they both heard it.

"Romeo." Her voice. Her own voice, hoarse and low and practical, pitched for a floor. "It's getting dark. Come up now. I've got the blanket. It's only me."

He felt Ling Mei go still against him, all of her, the way a hare goes still. Her good hand closed on the scruff of his neck.

The thread moved.

It did not pull. It *lifted*, gently, the way you lift a lead to remind a dog it is there, and something behind his breastbone that was shaped like a word leaned toward the hut. Not toward the voice; he would have known that and refused it. Toward the floor. Toward the den. Toward his own trough and his own tunnels and the round warm chamber he had dug with his own paws, where something was waiting at the top of the shaft for him to come home.

**\[Registered name "Romeo": called along binding.\]**  
**\[Holder: unnamed. Position: four paces.\]**

He went flat. It is hard to go flat inside a coat, but a dachshund can do it anywhere; he spread his short legs against Ling Mei's ribs and laid his chin on her collarbone and made himself as heavy as he knew how, and the lifting stopped, and the word behind his breastbone settled back where it belonged.

"That's mine," said Ling Mei, very softly. She was not talking about him. "That's my *floor*."

Her hand was shaking on his scruff. Not the tremor. Something else.

⟨Ling Mei.⟩

"It's sitting on my board. In my voice. Calling you down into your hole." She took a breath that went in jagged. "I'm going to go in there and..."

⟨And what?⟩

She did not have an answer to that. Neither did he, not one that ended well. You could not hit a hole. You could bite it, if you were very lucky and it was wearing something you knew better than it did, but it was wearing her, and he did not know yet which part of her it had.

⟨Listen to me,⟩ said Romeo. ⟨Do you know what it is doing?⟩

"Stealing my voice."

⟨It is lying at the mouth of my sett.⟩ He felt her go quiet, listening. ⟨That is what I do. A dachshund goes to the hole and lies down across it and waits for the badger to decide how much trouble it wants. It thinks I am down there. Or it thinks I will come home by the tunnel, the way I always do, and go to the trough because I am tired, and hear you calling, and come up the shaft.⟩

"You can't climb the shaft."

⟨It does not know that.⟩ He let that sit a moment. ⟨It knows a great deal about me. It knows the river and the hat and how Shen said my name. It does not know about my back. It does not know you carry me. It has been sitting on that board all day being you, and it has not once guessed that the real one would come in from the wrong side, on her feet, with the dog in her coat.⟩

Ling Mei's breath went out slowly, in a cloud.

"So it's in the wrong place."

⟨It is in the place it thinks I am,⟩ said Romeo. ⟨Which is the same thing. Let it keep the board warm.⟩

It was very hard for her. He could smell how hard: anger like hot iron, and under it something worse, the cold small horror of hearing your own voice be kind in a way you never are. But she buttoned the coat up over him, and she backed out from behind the woodpile on her knees in the snow, and she did not look at her door again.

---

The physician's room was at the far end of the kennels, past the last stall, past the fox's cage with its silver hasp: a narrow space that had once been a tack room and still smelled of old leather under the bitter herbs. He opened the door before Ling Mei knocked. Smoke was round his neck like a scarf, and the stoat's black eyes went straight to the front of her coat.

⟨You look terrible,⟩ said Smoke.

⟨I have been over a mountain twice,⟩ said Romeo. ⟨Tell him my back is bad and I am hungry and there is something in her hut.⟩

The physician listened to the stoat with his head on one side, and his one good eye went from Romeo to Ling Mei and back.

"Sit," he said to her. "Give him to me. Gently. No, keep his spine along your arm. Like that."

He laid Romeo on a folded horse blanket on the bench and went over his back with fingers that smelled of camphor and were much stronger than they looked. Romeo bore it. He bore it badly, with his lip lifting off his teeth twice and a sound in his chest he would have denied making, but he bore it, and at the end the old man straightened up and let out a long breath through his beard.

"Well," said the physician. "Nobody will need to lie for you tonight. I am to tell the Elder you are ill. You are ill."

He heated a bag of coarse salt on his little brazier until it hissed and laid it along the length of Romeo's back, over the red saddle, below the white band at his hips, and the heat went into the knotted places like a key into a lock. Then he did something better. He ladled out a small clay dish of millet gruel with a spoon of mutton fat stirred through it and set it down in front of Romeo's nose without a word.

Romeo ate it. He did not eat it with dignity. He ate it the way he had eaten things when he was young and the only kind of dog there was, with his ears in the dish and his eyes half closed, and when it was gone he licked the dish until it squeaked on the bench and then licked the bench.

**\[Condition: spinal strain, aggravated. Treatment: applied.\]**  
**\[Hunger: relieved.\]**  
**\[Rest recommended.\]**

*Recommend it to the stone,* thought Romeo, but without heat. The salt bag was very warm.

The physician bound Ling Mei's wrist while Smoke relayed, in short and rude bursts, what was sitting on her floor. The old man's fingers stopped once, halfway round the bandage, and then went on.

"It has been answering at her door all day," he said at last, to nobody in particular. "And the Elder sent to me at noon to say: be at his room at the bell, with the dog. He told the boy the girl was resting. He'd heard it from the boy, who'd heard it through the door." He tied off the bandage. "So in the eyes of this sect you have been home since dawn, being quiet and sensible, and talking kindly to your animal."

"I've never talked kindly to him," said Ling Mei.

"No," said the physician, "I didn't imagine you had."

Outside, very faintly, the light changed. The narrow window over the bench went from grey to the colour of weak tea, and somewhere out in the yard, across the snow, chains began to move. Not soundless ones. Ordinary iron, being dragged, and under it the low unhappy noise of wolves being walked to posts.

The dusk drum began on the wall: one stroke, a long gap, another.

**\[Quest: Nine Days to Stop Being a Puppy.\]**  
**\[The night has begun.\]**

The physician reached for his coat. Ling Mei reached for Romeo. And before either of them had finished, someone knocked on the tack-room door: three light, polite knocks, and then a pause, as if the knocker were giving them time.

Smoke went flat round the old man's neck. Romeo did not need to be told. He smelled it under the door: clean skin, faint silk, lamp smoke, a great deal of silver.

"Forgive me," said a light, pleasant voice. "I'm told the girl's little dog is ill. I wondered how he was sleeping."

The physician looked at Ling Mei. Ling Mei looked at Romeo. And Romeo, lying on the horse blanket with a hot salt bag on his back and a dish he had licked clean, smelled something under the door that he had not smelled from the collector once since he came, not at the gate, not at the stone, not in the yard: the soft, dry, faintly sour smell of grey kid leather being drawn slowly off a hand.
